# Frame Contestation in the Nation of Islam, 1952–1977
### A Diagnostic–Prognostic–Motivational Frame Analysis with Critical-Realist Retroduction

**Corpus base:** 34 primary documents selected from `1952-1977 - Contestation/` and `Initial Pilot Sampling/`, chosen for centrality, comparability across time, and direct bearing on the Elijah Muhammad–Malcolm X contestation and its 1975 aftermath in W. D. Muhammad's succession. Full list in **Works Cited**, below. Louis Farrakhan's 1978–1982 restorationist speeches are referenced only in the epilogue, outside the 1952–1977 evidentiary window, per Evidentiary Rule 4 (no retrospective contamination).

---

# PART I: DIAGNOSTIC FRAME ANALYSIS

Diagnostic frames answer: *what is wrong, and who or what caused it?* Three diagnostic frames are load-bearing across the corpus.

## D1 — The Devil-by-Nature Frame (Yacub's History)

### Frame Name
The White Man as Devil by Nature / Yacub's History

### Definition
The condition of Black subordination is diagnosed as the deliberate product of a genetically engineered "devil" race — the product of the scientist Yacub's eugenics experiment — who rules by "tricknology" rather than superior merit.

### Core Diagnosis
The cause of Black suffering is not circumstantial or merely historical; it is *ontological*. Whiteness is diagnosed as a created biological category with an inherent moral valence ("devil"), not a social position. This converts a political-economic grievance into a cosmological one.

### Problem Representation
The problem is represented as a hidden truth that has been concealed from the "so-called Negro" by the very devil-class that benefits from the concealment — hence the persistent phrase "so-called Negro," which itself diagnoses a *naming* problem (a false identity) prior to any material problem.

### Representative Quotations
- "In order to make the American so-called Negroes worship the devils, the American devils paint Jesus, God and the Angels white... so-called Negroes have worshipped the devils (white race) all of their lives." (*The True History of Jesus' Birth, Death, and What It Means to You and Me*, 1957)
- "We cannot hope for justice from the devils when by nature there [is none]... devils have you afraid and worshipping that which you know not." (*Our Saviour Has Arrived*, 1974)
- "The devil certainly did a 100 per cent job in turning the people against" their own nature and food (*How to Eat to Live*, Book 1, 1967)

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
Because the diagnosis is ontological rather than circumstantial, it forecloses reform of the relationship and licenses only separation (P1) as a logically consistent prognosis — reform of a "devil by nature" is not represented as available.

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Feeds directly into the Chosen-Nation/Divine-Urgency motivational frame (M1): if the antagonist's nature is fixed and cosmological, urgency is not contingent on political circumstance but on prophetic time.

### Empirical Level
Directly stated, repeatedly, across *The Supreme Wisdom* (1957), *The True History of Jesus* (1957), *Message to the Blackman in America* (1965), *How to Eat to Live* (1967), and *The Fall of America* (1973) — a seventeen-year documentary span under a single author, indicating this is not an early or transitional formulation but the constant doctrinal floor of the movement.

### Actual Level
The frame functions to relocate the source of injury from a contingent historical relationship (which could be renegotiated) to an immutable created nature (which cannot). This is the actual work the frame performs in the texts, independent of whether members took the cosmology literally in every instance.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Analytical inference, not direct evidence:* the frame's endurance for at least seventeen years, unmodified, across doctrinal restatement in five separate books, is consistent with (though not proof of) a generative mechanism in which the frame's organizational utility — as an unfalsifiable, non-negotiable boundary marker separating movement insiders from a hostile external world — outweighed any pressure toward doctrinal accommodation. This is offered as a plausible underlying mechanism, not an established fact; the corpus provides no direct testimony from Elijah Muhammad explaining the frame's function *as* a boundary-maintenance device.

### Relative Importance
**High.** It is the deepest and most temporally stable diagnostic frame in the corpus and the one from which Malcolm X's post-1964 departure is most sharply visible (see Part IV).

### Evidence Across Corpus
Present in TSWV (1957), TTHOJ (1957), MTTBIA (1965), HTETL (1967), TFOA (1973), OSHA (1974) — i.e., present at both endpoints of the 1952–1977 window and at every sampled midpoint. Absent, by contrast, from Malcolm X's 1964 Mecca letter and OAAU founding charter (see D2, D3 below), and explicitly reversed in W. D. Muhammad's 1975 interview (Part IV).

## D2 — Racial-Political Oppression (Victims of Americanism)

### Frame Name
Structural/Political Diagnosis: 400 Years of Slavery and Present Political Exclusion

### Definition
The condition of Black Americans is diagnosed as the product of ongoing, identifiable political mechanisms — voter suppression, gerrymandering, Dixiecrat control of Congress, economic exploitation — rather than (or, in Elijah Muhammad's texts, in addition to) a cosmological order.

### Core Diagnosis
Malcolm X, in the 1964 "Ballot or Bullet" address, diagnoses the problem in *institutional* rather than ontological terms: specific Senate committee structures, specific legislators, specific electoral mechanics.

### Problem Representation
"I'm one of the 22 million black people who are the victims of Americanism... victims of democracy, nothing but disguised hypocrisy" — the problem is represented as a systemic failure of stated democratic principle, not a fixed metaphysical order.

### Representative Quotations
- "The only reason the Dixiecrats control these committees is because they have seniority. The only reason they have seniority is because they come from states where Negroes can't vote." (Malcolm X, "The Ballot or the Bullet," April 3, 1964)
- "You and I in America are faced not with a segregationist conspiracy, we're faced with a government conspiracy." (same speech)
- Elijah Muhammad's own texts also invoke this register: "Separation of the so-called Negroes from their slave-master's children is a MUST" is preceded, in the same book, by extended argument about compounded, uncompensated labor ("Message to the Blackman in America," 1965) — showing the political-economic diagnosis is present in Elijah Muhammad's corpus too, but subordinated to and ultimately explained by the cosmological diagnosis (D1).

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
This diagnosis is compatible with *multiple, competing* prognoses — electoral mobilization (P2), international human-rights appeal (P3), or separation (P1) — which is precisely why it becomes the site of the sharpest prognostic contestation after 1964 (see Part IV).

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Underwrites the urgency frame differently than D1: "It isn't that time is running out — time has run out!" is a *this-worldly* political urgency, contrasted with M1's prophetic/eschatological urgency.

### Empirical Level
Directly stated in Malcolm X's April 1964 speech at length, with specific institutional detail (named senators, named committees, vote counts).

### Actual Level
The frame does the work of making Black political action legible as a *rights claim within* the American constitutional order ("The Constitution of the United States of America clearly affirms the right of every American citizen to bear arms" — invoked, notably, as a citizen's right, not as membership renounced) — a markedly different operation from D1, which places the audience *outside* that order altogether ("I don't even consider myself an American").

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the co-presence, in the same April 1964 speech, of both "I don't even consider myself an American" and citation of the U.S. Constitution as a source of a right to self-defense, is consistent with a transitional generative structure — a diagnosis in the process of being reworked rather than a settled position — rather than a stable synthesis. The corpus does not permit certainty about which element Malcolm X regarded as primary; both readings are argued in the secondary literature, and this report flags the tension as evidence of instability rather than resolving it.

### Relative Importance
**High** for the post-1963 period specifically; the frame is present but subordinate in Elijah Muhammad's corpus throughout.

### Evidence Across Corpus
"Ballot or the Bullet" (1964); OAAU founding charter (June 1964, invoking "the Charter of the United Nations, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the Constitution of the United States and the Bill of Rights" as documents "we believe" represent legitimate principle); present in muted, subordinated form in MTTBIA (1965).

## D3 — Mental Slavery / Christian False Consciousness

### Frame Name
The So-Called Negro's Graveyard: Religious and Educational Brainwashing

### Definition
The condition of Black subordination is diagnosed as, in significant part, a problem of internalized belief — a "mental" or "spiritual" captivity produced by Christian religious formation and white-authored education, prior to and enabling the material subordination.

### Core Diagnosis
"The Bible is the graveyard of my poor people (the so-called Negroes)" (TSWV, 1957) diagnoses the problem as epistemic: the wrong sacred text, the wrong self-concept, producing complicity in one's own subordination.

### Problem Representation
Malcolm X extends precisely this diagnosis into secular education: "Our children are being criminally shortchanged in the public school system... The textbooks tell our children nothing about the great contributions of Afro-Americans" (OAAU Founding Rally, June 1964) — a structurally identical diagnosis (false knowledge produces false self-conception) applied to a different institution (public schools rather than the Black church).

### Representative Quotations
- "The Bible, church and Christianity have deceived the so-called Negroes." (TSWV, 1957)
- "Every little child going to school thinks his grandfather was a cotton picker. Why, your grandfather was Nat Turner... your grandfather was Hannibal." (Malcolm X, OAAU Founding Rally, 1964)
- W. D. Muhammad, in the 1975 first interview, retrospectively diagnoses this same phenomenon among Christian Black Americans of the 1930s: "black people were almost 100% Christian... You didn't have to go to Church to be born with a Christian mind" — treating pre-NOI Black Christian consciousness itself as the object requiring diagnosis and correction (also present, independently, in the Fard-to-Muhammad 1933 letter's injunction to "give life to the dead Nation of Islam").

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
This diagnosis licenses an *educational/pedagogical* prognosis distinct from either separation (P1) or electoral politics (P2): the Muslim school system, OAAU's community-controlled schools, and W. D. Muhammad's later doctrinal correction all follow from D3 rather than D1 or D2 alone.

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Feeds a distinct motivational register — not urgency toward an external enemy, but urgency toward self-transformation ("It starts first with self," in Farrakhan's 1978 restatement, outside the strict window but continuous with this frame's logic).

### Empirical Level
Directly and repeatedly stated across Elijah Muhammad's corpus (1957–1974) and independently, in structurally parallel form, in Malcolm X's 1964 OAAU material.

### Actual Level
The frame does the work of making leadership itself (of Elijah Muhammad, later contested by and between Malcolm X's OAAU and W. D. Muhammad's reformed NOI) indispensable: if the primary problem is false consciousness, only an authoritative corrective teacher — not merely a policy change — can resolve it. This underwrites the intense personalization of authority visible throughout the corpus.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the frame's independent emergence in both Elijah Muhammad's religious register and Malcolm X's later secular-educational register suggests a shared underlying logic of "corrective pedagogy against internalized subordination" that outlasted the personal schism between the two men — i.e., the schism (Part IV) operated at the level of prognosis and loyalty far more than at the level of this diagnostic structure, which both continued to deploy. This is an inference about a shared deep structure, not a claim that either man acknowledged the parallel.

### Relative Importance
**Medium-High.** Present throughout, but functions more as a supporting diagnosis beneath D1 (in Elijah Muhammad's corpus) or D2 (in Malcolm X's later corpus) than as a freestanding master diagnosis.

### Evidence Across Corpus
TSWV (1957); OAAU Founding Charter (1964); W. D. Muhammad, First Official Interview (March 1975).

# PART II: PROGNOSTIC FRAME ANALYSIS

Prognostic frames answer: *what should be done, and by what mechanism will it work?*

## P1 — Divine Separation

### Frame Name
Separation as the Only Solution

### Definition
The prescribed solution to D1 (and, derivatively, D2 and D3) is territorial, economic, and social separation of Black Americans from White America — not reform, not integration, not coexistence.

### Problem Addressed
D1 directly: if the antagonist is devil-by-nature, no corrective relationship with that antagonist is possible; only exit is coherent.

### Proposed Solution
"Separation of the so-called Negroes from their slave-master's children is a MUST. It is the only... Allah, God, is calling for a separation!" (MTTBIA, 1965). *The Fall of America* (1973) restates this as prophecy: "The Fall of America Foretold, Separation Is the Answer."

### Mechanism of Change
The mechanism is theological rather than organizational or political: separation is represented as divinely mandated and prophetically inevitable ("this is the time of our separation and the judgment of this world"), not as a policy outcome contingent on negotiation, legislation, or coalition-building. This is a critical structural difference from P2 and P3 below, both of which specify concrete organizational mechanisms (voter registration, self-defense committees, international appeal).

### Representative Quotations
- "You must know that this is the time of our separation and the judgment of this world." (MTTBIA, 1965)
- "Watch how anxious the white man is to hold you and call you by his name." (MTTBIA, 1965 — separation is framed as being obstructed by the antagonist's self-interested resistance, reinforcing its necessity)

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Directly derived from D1; largely incompatible with D2 read as a claim to full American citizenship rights, since separation abandons rather than claims the American polity.

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Bound tightly to M1 (Divine Urgency): separation is not optional strategy but a commanded, time-bound response to prophecy.

### Empirical Level
Stated directly and repeatedly, 1957–1974, unmodified over seventeen years.

### Actual Level
Functions to justify the Nation of Islam's parallel economic institutions (farms, businesses, schools) documented in *Muhammad Speaks* and the 1975 "Accomplishments of the Muslims" pamphlet — i.e., the frame is not merely rhetorical but corresponds to an observable pattern of institution-building oriented toward self-sufficient Black economic infrastructure rather than integration into existing white-owned or white-governed institutions.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the frame's stability alongside genuine, resource-intensive institution-building (documented in the corpus's economic self-reporting) suggests separation functioned as more than rhetoric — it is consistent with an underlying organizational strategy of building parallel institutional capacity as insurance against both white hostility and internal defection, though the corpus does not contain direct strategic documents confirming this as a stated organizational rationale.

### Relative Importance
**High** throughout Elijah Muhammad's leadership; **directly reversed** by his son and successor in 1975 (see Part IV), which is itself the single clearest instance of prognostic frame discontinuity in the corpus.

### Evidence Across Corpus
MTTBIA (1965), TFOA (1973); economic self-reporting in "Accomplishments of the Muslims" (1975).

### Organisational Significance
Separation as prognosis directly organizes movement resources toward land acquisition, farming, and business ownership rather than toward electoral mobilization or coalition politics — a divergence from Malcolm X's post-1964 organizational choices (OAAU) that is a *direct, demonstrable* consequence of prognostic disagreement, not merely rhetorical difference.

### Transformational Orientation

**Individual transformation:** required (dietary law, "How to Eat to Live," moral discipline) as a precondition of collective separation, but not sufficient alone.

**Collective transformation:** the primary target — the "Black Nation" as a corporate unit is the object to be separated and reconstituted.

**Institutional transformation:** extensive — parallel schools, farms, and businesses, documented directly in the corpus's self-published accounts.

**Wider social transformation:** explicitly *not* sought with respect to American society as a whole; TFOA (1973) frames American society's transformation only as collapse ("the Fall"), not as a reformable object.

## P2 — Political Self-Determination / The Ballot

### Frame Name
Electoral and Institutional Self-Determination Within America

### Definition
The prescribed solution, in Malcolm X's 1964 "Ballot or Bullet" address, is Black voter registration, bloc voting, and community control of local institutions (schools, in particular) as the near-term mechanism of change — explicitly offered as an alternative to (not yet, at that date, a replacement for) separation.

### Problem Addressed
D2 directly: since the diagnosed problem is institutional exclusion from a functioning democratic mechanism, the prescribed solution is inclusion in and capture of that mechanism.

### Proposed Solution
Community control of the 10% of Harlem/Bedford-Stuyvesant schools the Board of Education admitted it could not improve; bloc voting to "determine who's going to sit in the White House."

### Mechanism of Change
The mechanism is explicitly procedural and institutional: expulsion of Dixiecrat committee chairs under existing constitutional machinery, community takeover of specific underperforming schools, textbook reform — each identified with a specific institutional lever, not a prophetic timetable.

### Representative Quotations
- "It's either a ballot or a bullet... it's time now for you and me to become more politically mature and realize what the ballot is for."
- "Let them turn those schools over to us. Since they say they can't handle them, nor can they correct them, let us take a whack at it."

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Derived from D2 and D3 jointly (institutional exclusion plus miseducation).

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Bound to a *conditional* urgency: "the ballot, or... the bullet" is explicitly framed as contingent on whether the ballot mechanism is allowed to function — a conditional-branching urgency structurally distinct from M1's unconditional prophetic urgency.

### Empirical Level
Stated directly, at length, with institutional specificity, in the April 1964 speech.

### Actual Level
The speech functions to reposition Malcolm X, only weeks after his departure from the Nation of Islam, as a figure addressing a broad, religiously mixed Black audience ("Baptist, or a Methodist, or a Muslim, or a nationalist") on grounds of shared racial interest rather than shared theology — a substantive prognostic and rhetorical departure from anything found in the Elijah Muhammad corpus, where audience address is consistently theological ("so-called Negroes... accept the understanding").

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the shift from theological to civic-political address occurring within weeks of the organizational break is consistent with (not proof of) a generative process in which loss of the Nation of Islam's institutional and doctrinal authority compelled Malcolm X to construct a new basis of address — shared racial-political interest — capable of aggregating a following independent of NOI theology. The corpus supports this as a plausible mechanism; it does not contain direct first-person testimony from Malcolm X explaining the address shift as strategically necessitated by the break.

### Relative Importance
**High** for a brief but pivotal period (March–June 1964), superseded within the same year by P3.

### Evidence Across Corpus
"The Ballot or the Bullet" (April 3, 1964) — the frame's clearest and essentially only full articulation in the sampled corpus.

## P3 — Pan-African / Human-Rights Internationalization

### Frame Name
Organization of Afro-American Unity: Human Rights Beyond U.S. Civil Rights

### Definition
Following the Mecca pilgrimage (April 1964), the prescribed solution shifts from domestic electoral mechanics (P2) toward international human-rights appeal and Pan-African unity modeled explicitly on the Organization of African Unity (est. Addis Ababa, 1963).

### Problem Addressed
A reformulated version of D2: the problem is redefined from "civil rights" (a domestic legal category) to "human rights" (an international category), explicitly to permit appeal to bodies and audiences outside U.S. domestic jurisdiction.

### Proposed Solution
"The Organization of Afro-American Unity... patterned after the letter and spirit of the Organization of African Unity"; unification of "all people of African descent in the Western Hemisphere... with our brothers on the African continent."

### Mechanism of Change
Coalition-building among African postcolonial states and Afro-diasporic communities to internationalize what had been treated as a purely domestic American question — explicitly analogized to the OAU's success (per Malcolm X) in achieving in roughly a decade more "independence" and "recognition" than Black Americans had achieved in a much longer span.

### Representative Quotations
- "We determined to try and do the same thing here in America among Afro-Americans... to fight whoever gets in our way, to bring about the complete independence of people of African descent... by any means necessary."
- Mecca letter: "America needs to understand Islam, because this is the one religion that erases from its society the race problem" — the universalist theological reframing (see D-level shift, Part IV) that underwrites this prognosis's departure from D1's fixed racial cosmology.

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Explicitly *severed* from D1: the Mecca letter's testimony that "the 'white' Muslims" showed "the same sincerity" as African Muslims is diagnostically incompatible with a devil-by-nature account of whiteness. This is the corpus's single clearest documented instance of diagnostic frame transformation within one individual's public record (see Part IV).

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Introduces a new motivational register — universal brotherhood under monotheism (M3) — that has no equivalent in Elijah Muhammad's corpus in the sampled period.

### Empirical Level
Directly stated in the Mecca letter (April 20, 1964) and the OAAU founding charter (June 28, 1964), separated by roughly nine weeks — indicating rapid frame revision following direct experiential contact with the Hajj, rather than gradual doctrinal evolution.

### Actual Level
Functions to reposition the movement's claim-making audience from the U.S. federal government (P2's target) to the community of postcolonial African states and international human-rights bodies — a change in the addressee of the claim, not merely its content.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the tight nine-week interval between the Mecca letter's diagnostic reversal on whiteness and the OAAU charter's prognostic internationalization suggests the diagnostic change was generative of (causally prior to) the prognostic change, rather than the reverse — i.e., the corpus is more consistent with "new direct experience of non-racialized Islam prompted new strategy" than with "new strategic calculation prompted post-hoc theological revision." This is inference from sequence and content, not direct testimony to that effect; Malcolm X's own retrospective explanation of the causal order is not present in the sampled documents.

### Relative Importance
**High** for the final ten months of Malcolm X's public life; **absent** from the Elijah Muhammad corpus at any sampled date.

### Evidence Across Corpus
Mecca Letter (April 20, 1964); OAAU Founding Charter (June 28, 1964).

## P4 — Reform and Reintegration (W. D. Muhammad, 1975)

### Frame Name
Universalization and Domestic Reintegration

### Definition
Upon succession in February 1975, W. D. Muhammad prescribes the dissolution of racial exclusivity in membership and doctrine, reintegration with orthodox international Islam, and a de-emphasis (not full repudiation, in his own careful framing) of confrontational racial rhetoric.

### Problem Addressed
Reframes D1 as no longer operative: "the past is the past, unless it is dug up. We will not dig it up." The devil-by-nature diagnosis is not argued against point-by-point; it is set aside as belonging to a completed historical phase of the mission.

### Proposed Solution
"We do not turn to the East for leadership... [but] all Muslims are brothers. Our relationship will be that of one independent community of Muslims with other independent communities of Muslims" — explicit normalization of relations with orthodox Sunni Islam, and: "Whites will even be permitted to join the sect" (as reported externally in *Time*, June 1975, corroborating the domestic press's independent observation of the same shift W. D. Muhammad describes in his own words).

### Mechanism of Change
The mechanism is presented as *interpretive succession* rather than doctrinal rupture: W. D. Muhammad frames himself as a "Mujeddid" ("one to watch over the new Islam... to see that it is constantly and continually being renewed"), i.e., the mechanism of change is authorized continuity of interpretive office, not repudiation of the founder.

### Representative Quotations
- "A Messenger or a Divinely guided man has a responsibility to deliver a message and point to direction and signs. You point out an enemy to focus attention on the enemy. When that attention has been given and the enemy has begun to reform... it would be uncivilized and certainly un-Godly to stay with the finger pointing violently at the enemy."
- "The name Black Muslim... he rejects it as a racist label, but accepts it as an identifying term."

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Retains D3 (mental/religious captivity) as still operative and still requiring correction, while setting aside D1 — a selective, not wholesale, diagnostic revision. This distinction is significant and is easy to overstate in either direction; the corpus supports "D1 de-emphasized and historically bounded" as direct evidence, but does not support a stronger claim that W. D. Muhammad repudiated the founder's theology outright — he explicitly declines to "dig up" the past rather than denouncing it.

### Connection to Motivational Frames
Replaces M1's eschatological urgency with an institution-building and legitimacy-seeking motivational register: "Will you seek financial help from the city and state..." "Yes, just as we have already done" — urgency is now directed at the mundane task of institutional survival and civic standing, not apocalyptic timing.

### Empirical Level
Directly stated across three sampled 1975 documents (First Official Interview, March 21; "I Was Born for This Mission," same interview under alternate title; Saviors Day address, February 26).

### Actual Level
Functions, observably, to reposition the organization for external legitimacy: the *Time* report (June 1975) independently corroborates that this reframing produced concrete effects — invitations to "white civic and church leaders," appeals "for white money" — i.e., the frame is not merely internal doctrine but is oriented toward, and achieves, changed relations with external institutions within months.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the near-immediate (within 100 days of succession) and externally visible character of this reframing is consistent with a generative mechanism in which succession crisis and institutional-financial pressure (the corpus's own report of the movement's substantial property holdings, $14.5 million in Chicago property, at the point of transition) created strong incentive toward legitimation strategies compatible with mainstream civic and financial institutions. This is a plausible mechanism grounded in the corpus's own financial self-disclosure, but the corpus does not contain internal deliberative documents (board minutes, private correspondence) that would allow this to rise above inference.

### Relative Importance
**High**, and represents the corpus's second major instance (after Malcolm X, 1964) of full diagnostic-prognostic frame transformation by a principal leader — but achieved *without* organizational schism, in contrast to 1964.

### Evidence Across Corpus
W. D. Muhammad, Saviors Day Address Pt. 2 (Feb. 26, 1975); First Official Interview (March 21, 1975); *Time*, "Religion: White Muslims?" (June 30, 1975).

# PART III: MOTIVATIONAL FRAME ANALYSIS

Motivational frames answer: *why should one act now, and on what basis is continued commitment demanded?*

## M1 — Chosen Nation / Divine Urgency

### Frame Name
Prophetic Urgency and the Chosen Nation

### Definition
Commitment and action are demanded on the basis that the audience is a specifically chosen people ("Lost-Found Nation of Islam," "tribe of Shabazz") living at a divinely fixed and imminent turning point ("the ending of the world... the world of the white man").

### Core Motivational Logic
Time is not open-ended; it is prophetically bounded, and the movement's teacher possesses privileged, divinely sourced knowledge of its schedule. Action is owed not to strategic calculation but to obedience within a cosmic drama already underway.

### Representative Quotations
- "There is much talk... about the ending of the world that we now live in (the world of the white man). The white race is losing their divine power to rule." (Elijah Muhammad, response to Malcolm X's departure, *Muhammad Speaks*, September 11, 1964)
- Fard Muhammad to Elijah Muhammad, 1933: "Go right ahead with teaching righteousness to our own nation. Allah is with you... Your happiness is awaiting you on completion of your labor" — urgency framed as personal mission under direct divine commission from the movement's very founding document in the corpus.

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Directly derived from D1: only a fixed, cosmologically-ordered antagonist permits a fixed, prophetically-scheduled resolution.

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
Bound to P1 (separation): "this is the time of our separation and the judgment of this world" collapses the distinction between strategic timing and prophetic timing entirely.

### Empirical Level
Present from the corpus's earliest document (1933) through its latest Elijah Muhammad-authored document (1974) — a forty-one-year span, the longest continuity of any frame identified in this analysis.

### Actual Level
Functions to insulate the movement's authority structure from ordinary political contingency: setbacks, defections, and legal difficulties (documented independently in the FBI files, below) are represented as expected features of prophetic time rather than as evidence against leadership.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the frame's extraordinary temporal stability, unmodified across a documented external shock as severe as Malcolm X's public departure and later assassination, suggests the frame's function was, in part, to render such shocks doctrinally unsurprising and thus non-threatening to continued membership commitment. This is inferred from the frame's behavior under strain, not from any statement in the corpus explicitly describing this as the frame's purpose.

### Relative Importance
**High** throughout Elijah Muhammad's leadership; **directly and explicitly** de-emphasized (not eliminated) under W. D. Muhammad in 1975, who redirects motivational language toward institution-building urgency rather than eschatological urgency (see P4).

### Evidence Across Corpus
Fard letter (1933); TSWV (1957); response to Malcolm X's departure (1964); TFOA (1973); OSHA (1974).

## M2 — Loyalty and Hypocrisy: The Discourse of the Defector

### Frame Name
Betrayal, Hypocrisy, and the Boundary of Legitimate Voice

### Definition
Commitment is demanded and enforced through a sharply binary discourse distinguishing loyal, legitimate spokesmen from "hypocrites" and "defectors" — a category applied with particular force and specificity to Malcolm X after March 1964.

### Core Motivational Logic
The frame does not merely describe disagreement; it delegitimizes the very act of public dissent by an insider as a moral (not merely a strategic) betrayal, converting policy or theological disagreement into a question of personal loyalty and even mortal desert.

### Representative Quotations
- Louis X (later Louis Farrakhan), December 4, 1964: describing Malcolm X as "defector and hypocrite Malcolm" whose "shameful attempts... to slander and destroy the great Islamic leader" had "caused concern not only among the ranks of Muslims, but among many fair-minded non-Muslims."
- Same article: "Such a man as Malcolm is made... Worthy of death, and would have met with death if it had not been for Muhammad's confidence in Allah for victory over the enemies."
- Elijah Muhammad's own September 1964 restatement of doctrine, published in direct response to Malcolm X's departure, does not name him but reasserts, at length and without concession, the very doctrines Malcolm X had just publicly begun to depart from — itself a motivational act of reaffirmed loyalty-demand through doctrinal restatement rather than direct personal address.

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
The "hypocrite" label functions as a diagnostic category in its own right — it diagnoses the defector's stated reasons for leaving as pretextual, thereby foreclosing engagement with the substance of Malcolm X's post-departure diagnostic revisions (D2, D3 reweighted; eventual rejection of D1).

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
Functions to discredit, by association with the discredited person, any prognosis (P2, P3) that Malcolm X subsequently proposed, independent of that prognosis's substantive content.

### Empirical Level
Directly documented in Louis X's December 1964 article — including the explicit "worthy of death" language, published in *Muhammad Speaks* roughly ten weeks before Malcolm X's assassination on February 21, 1965.

### Actual Level
This is a point requiring particular evidentiary caution (Evidentiary Rule 9, Negative Evidence). The corpus contains a documented published statement describing Malcolm X as "worthy of death" prior to his assassination. **This is direct textual evidence of the existence and publication of such language.** It is **not**, on the strength of this corpus alone, evidence of a causal link between this specific publication and the assassination itself, which involved separate individuals, a criminal investigation, and a historical record outside the scope of the sampled documents. This report records the frame's documented content and its documented timing; it does not draw a causal inference the corpus cannot support.

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference, clearly bounded:* the intensity and personalization of this loyalty-enforcement language in late 1964 is consistent with a generative mechanism in which the movement's authority structure, built substantially on the personalized charismatic and prophetic frames above (M1), had few available registers for absorbing public, doctrinally-grounded dissent from a former senior minister other than total moral delegitimization of the dissenter. This is offered strictly as one plausible mechanism explaining the frame's severity, not as an account of intent or of any specific actor's motives regarding the events of February 1965.

### Relative Importance
**High** specifically at the moment of schism (1964); the frame recedes, without being formally repudiated, under the reintegrative approach of 1975 ("we will not dig it up").

### Evidence Across Corpus
Louis X, "Malcolm... Biggest Hypocrite of 1964" (*Muhammad Speaks*, Dec. 4, 1964); Elijah Muhammad's September 1964 doctrinal restatement.

---

## M3 — Universal Brotherhood Under One God

### Frame Name
Post-Racial Monotheistic Fellowship

### Definition
Commitment is grounded not in racial chosen-ness (M1) but in the experience of a religiously constituted brotherhood that explicitly crosses the racial line the movement had previously treated as absolute.

### Core Motivational Logic
Direct, embodied experience ("I have eaten from the same plate, drunk from the same glass, and slept in the same bed... with fellow Muslims, whose eyes were the bluest of blue") is offered as motivationally superior to received doctrine — experience overturns prior teaching.

### Representative Quotations
- "We were truly all the same (brothers)—because their belief in one God had removed the 'white' from their minds." (Malcolm X, Mecca Letter, April 20, 1964)
- W. D. Muhammad, 1975, extending the same logic domestically rather than internationally: "if the white man respects us, we will respect him" (as reported in *Time*, June 1975, and consistent with his own recorded statements on ending the stress on "the white man's past atrocities").

### Connection to Diagnostic Frames
Directly incompatible with D1 and requires either its abandonment (Malcolm X, W. D. Muhammad) or its careful historical bounding ("the past is the past... we will not dig it up," W. D. Muhammad) rather than its reassertion.

### Connection to Prognostic Frames
Underwrites P3 (Pan-African internationalization, which explicitly includes white sympathizers and international Muslim solidarity) and P4 (domestic reintegration).

### Empirical Level
Directly stated in the Mecca Letter (1964) and echoed, in a domestically-oriented register, in W. D. Muhammad's 1975 statements — an eleven-year gap between two independent, non-identical articulations of a structurally similar motivational shift by two different men, both of whom had a direct personal and institutional relationship to Elijah Muhammad.

### Actual Level
Functions to reconstitute the basis of belonging from ascribed racial status to shared religious experience or civic reconciliation — a change with direct organizational consequences in both cases (OAAU's stated inclusion of "brothers and sisters" defined by African descent rather than religious doctrine; W. D. Muhammad's stated willingness to admit white members).

### Real Level (Retroduction)
*Retroductive inference:* the appearance of structurally similar universalizing motivational shifts in two men whose personal relationship (mentor's son and estranged senior minister) was otherwise antagonistic, occurring roughly a decade apart under very different immediate circumstances (pilgrimage experience versus succession to leadership), is consistent with — but does not prove — a shared underlying pressure toward doctrinal universalization once movement figures gained direct, sustained contact with the international, non-racialized Muslim world (Mecca, in Malcolm X's case; correspondence and travel described in W. D. Muhammad's own 1975 account of his Hajj). This report treats this parallel as suggestive rather than demonstrated, since the corpus contains no direct statement from either man citing the other's prior articulation.

### Relative Importance
**High** at both points of emergence (1964; 1975); **absent** from Elijah Muhammad's own corpus in the sampled period.

### Evidence Across Corpus
Mecca Letter (1964); W. D. Muhammad, First Official Interview (1975); *Time* (1975).

# PART IV: LEADERSHIP TRANSITION AND FRAME CONTESTATION

## Elijah Muhammad
Across every sampled document from 1957 through 1974 (seventeen years, five separate books, and a direct doctrinal response to the 1964 schism), Elijah Muhammad's diagnostic (D1), prognostic (P1), and motivational (M1) frames are stable and mutually reinforcing. The devil-by-nature diagnosis licenses only separation as a coherent prognosis, and both are held together by an eschatological urgency that treats external shocks — including Malcolm X's departure — as confirmations of, rather than challenges to, the underlying frame ("there is much talk... about the ending of the world," published within weeks of the schism, restates doctrine rather than engaging Malcolm X's stated reasons for leaving).

## Malcolm X
Malcolm X's public record across 1964 alone documents a rapid, sequential, and directly evidenced frame transformation:
- **March–April 1964** ("Ballot or the Bullet"): D2 (political-institutional diagnosis) foregrounded; P2 (electoral/community-control prognosis) proposed; D1 not invoked.
- **April 20, 1964** (Mecca Letter): direct reversal of D1's core premise — the claim that whiteness is inherently corrupted is explicitly contradicted by testified experience of "white" Muslims' sincerity.
- **June 28, 1964** (OAAU Founding): P3 (Pan-African human-rights internationalization) formally organized, explicitly modeled on the OAU, incorporating both D2 and the post-Mecca revision of D1.

This is the corpus's clearest documented case of **rapid, sequential frame revision by a single named individual**, evidenced directly (not inferred) by dated, first-person documents produced within a nine-week window.

## Frame Continuity
Notably, D3 (the diagnosis of internalized false consciousness via inadequate education) is the one diagnostic frame that persists, in structurally parallel form, on both sides of the schism: Elijah Muhammad's critique of Christian religious formation and Malcolm X's critique of public-school textbooks perform the same diagnostic operation (false knowledge produces false self-conception) even as the two men diverge completely on D1 and on every prognostic frame. This is direct evidence that the schism was not a wholesale rejection of the movement's entire diagnostic apparatus by Malcolm X, but a selective and specific one.

## Frame Transformation
The evidence supports the following claim at the level of **analytical inference** (not merely direct restatement of the documents, but not rising to confirmed causal mechanism either): Malcolm X's 1964 frame transformation proceeds diagnosis-first — the Mecca experience produces a diagnostic revision (D1's reversal) that *precedes*, by weeks, the prognostic reorganization (P3/OAAU). This sequencing is directly evidenced by the dates of the two documents (April 20 and June 28) and their content; the causal claim that the former generated the latter is a retroductive inference consistent with, but not conclusively established by, this sequence alone.

## Frame Contestation
The corpus documents active, weaponized contestation, not merely passive divergence: Louis X's December 1964 characterization of Malcolm X as a "hypocrite" whose conduct made him "Worthy of death" is direct evidence of an organized, published motivational-frame response (M2) specifically targeting the legitimacy of Malcolm X's post-departure frames, published in the same newspaper (*Muhammad Speaks*) that had carried Elijah Muhammad's own more restrained September 1964 doctrinal restatement. The two documents together show a movement leadership response operating on two registers simultaneously: doctrinal reaffirmation (by Elijah Muhammad, addressed to the general membership) and personal delegitimization (by a senior minister, addressed at the departed figure directly).

## Leadership and Frame Alignment
W. D. Muhammad's 1975 succession represents a second, structurally similar frame transformation — again diagnosis-and-motivation-first (D1 quietly set aside; M1's eschatological urgency redirected toward institutional legitimacy) followed by prognostic reorganization (P4) — but achieved through **institutional succession** rather than personal departure and schism, and therefore *without* triggering the M2-type "hypocrite" delegitimization response that met Malcolm X. This is a directly observable structural contrast: the same category of frame change (abandonment of D1, redirection of M1) produced schism and lethal-register discourse in 1964 but produced institutional continuity in 1975. The corpus supports identifying this contrast; it does not, by itself, establish why authorized succession absorbed the change while unauthorized departure did not — that explanation would require additional evidence (on succession legitimacy, family relation to the founder, organizational control of the movement's press) beyond what this sampled corpus can settle with confidence.

# PART V: INTEGRATED FRAME RELATIONSHIPS

**Problem → Causal Interpretation → Proposed Solution → Mechanism of Change → Motivational Imperative → Expected Action**

**Elijah Muhammad, 1957–1974 (stable):**
Black subordination → caused by devil-by-nature antagonist (D1) → separation (P1) → divinely mandated, prophetically scheduled unfolding (mechanism = revelation/prophecy, not negotiation) → prophetic urgency, chosen-nation status (M1) → build parallel institutions; await/accelerate separation; maintain doctrinal and personal loyalty to the Messenger.

**Malcolm X, March 1964:**
Black subordination → institutional political exclusion (D2) → electoral mobilization, community school control (P2) → constitutional/procedural mechanisms → this-worldly conditional urgency ("ballot or bullet") → register to vote, organize bloc voting, demand community control.

**Malcolm X, April–June 1964 (post-Mecca):**
Black subordination → institutional political exclusion (D2), now explicitly *without* D1 → Pan-African human-rights internationalization (P3) → coalition-building modeled on OAU, international appeal → universal brotherhood (M3) plus continued urgency → build OAAU, unify Afro-diasporic peoples, appeal to international bodies.

**Louis X, December 1964:**
Movement legitimacy threatened by defection → defection diagnosed as moral hypocrisy, not substantive disagreement (M2 functioning diagnostically) → discredit the defector as the "solution" to the threat → mechanism = public delegitimization in the movement press → loyalty-and-betrayal urgency → reaffirm loyalty to Elijah Muhammad; treat Malcolm X's claims as void regardless of content.

**W. D. Muhammad, February–March 1975:**
Movement legitimacy and doctrinal direction contested by succession → D1 historically bounded, not repudiated; D3 retained → reintegration with orthodox Islam, admission of white members, civic legitimation (P4) → mechanism = authorized interpretive renewal ("Mujeddid") → institution-building and legitimacy-seeking urgency, replacing eschatology → normalize relations with government and orthodox Muslim world; retain, don't "dig up," the founder's legacy.

This sequence directly demonstrates, across a documented eleven-year span, at least three structurally distinct diagnostic-prognostic-motivational packages operating under the same organizational lineage, two of which (Malcolm X's, W. D. Muhammad's) explicitly reject the founding cosmological diagnosis (D1) while a third (Louis X's, at the moment of schism) intensifies loyalty-enforcement around that same diagnosis.

# PART VI: COMPARATIVE ASSESSMENT

## Diagnostic Frames
D1 (devil-by-nature) is the corpus's most temporally stable frame under Elijah Muhammad and the corpus's most sharply *contested* frame across the leadership transitions — it is the one point on which Malcolm X (1964) and W. D. Muhammad (1975) independently converge in departing from the founder's teaching, despite otherwise divergent prognoses (internationalization vs. domestic reintegration) and despite the intervening decade of hostility between the Malcolm X and Nation of Islam camps. D2 and D3 show far greater continuity across all documented positions, suggesting these were less doctrinally load-bearing and more available for common ground even amid schism.

## Prognostic Frames
The four identified prognoses (separation, electoral mobilization, internationalization, reintegration) are **not** presented in the corpus as compatible variants of a single strategy; they are documented as sequential *replacements*, each displacing the one before it in the relevant leader's public record (Malcolm X moved from P2 to P3 within roughly three months; W. D. Muhammad's P4 explicitly supersedes P1 upon succession). The corpus provides no example of any leader holding two of these prognoses simultaneously as complementary.

## Motivational Frames
M1 (prophetic urgency) and M3 (universal brotherhood) are mutually exclusive in the sampled corpus — no document combines a devil-by-nature-derived urgency with a universal-brotherhood motivational appeal. M2 (loyalty/hypocrisy discourse) is available to, and used by, defenders of *either* an M1-based or (implicitly, in later periods not sampled here) a reformed orthodoxy; it functions as a boundary-policing frame independent of which underlying diagnostic-prognostic package is being defended.

---

# PART VII: TEMPORAL DEVELOPMENT, 1952–1977

**1952–1959:** Foundational period. The earliest sampled document (Fard's 1933 letter, predating the analytical window but establishing its origin point) and Elijah Muhammad's 1957 books (*The Supreme Wisdom*, *The True History of Jesus*) establish D1, P1, and M1 essentially in the form they will retain for the following two decades. No sampled document from this sub-period shows any internal contestation.

**1960–1964:** Consolidation and growing public visibility (evidenced indirectly by the FBI's 1955–1958 files, which document sustained state surveillance interest predating the schism — see Part VIII). Elijah Muhammad's doctrinal corpus continues unmodified. Malcolm X remains, through early 1964, the movement's most visible public voice while privately and then publicly diverging.

**1964–1965:** The corpus's most concentrated period of documented frame contestation: Malcolm X's departure (March), "Ballot or the Bullet" (April 3), the Mecca Letter (April 20), the OAAU founding (June 28), Louis X's "hypocrite" article (December 4), and Malcolm X's assassination (February 21, 1965, outside the sampled documents but essential context for interpreting the preceding texts' stakes). No other eighteen-month span in the corpus contains a comparable density of directly evidenced, dated frame change and frame conflict.

**1965–1977:** Return to doctrinal stability under Elijah Muhammad (*How to Eat to Live*, 1967; *The Fall of America*, 1973; *Our Saviour Has Arrived*, 1974 — all restating D1/P1/M1 without modification), followed by a second concentrated period of frame transformation at his death: W. D. Muhammad's succession (February 1975) and the documented reintegrative reframing of March–June 1975, independently corroborated by external press coverage (*Time*, June 1975). The window closes (1977) roughly two years into this second transformation, before its longer-term consolidation (which the corpus does not sample) and before the subsequent restorationist countermovement associated with Louis Farrakhan's post-1977 activity, which lies outside this analysis's evidentiary boundary.

---

# PART VIII: RETRODUCTIVE ANALYSIS OF THE PERIOD

The following are offered explicitly as retroductive inferences concerning possible underlying generative mechanisms — not as established facts, and not as claims the sampled corpus alone can verify. They are flagged, per the evidentiary rules, as inference beyond direct textual evidence.

1. **A cosmological diagnosis (D1) that forecloses reform-based prognoses is organizationally self-reinforcing under stable leadership but becomes a specific point of vulnerability at moments of leadership transition**, because any successor's access to different lived experience (international travel, direct contact with a religiously and racially diverse Muslim world) creates the possibility of diagnostic revision that a purely domestic, insulated leadership environment would not generate. This is consistent with both Malcolm X's (1964) and W. D. Muhammad's (1975) independent departures from D1 following, in both documented cases, direct international/Hajj experience — but the corpus's sample size (two cases) is too small to elevate this beyond a plausible pattern.

2. **Loyalty-and-hypocrisy discourse (M2) intensifies in direct proportion to the doctrinal centrality of the frame being defended.** The corpus's single most severe instance of M2 (the "worthy of death" language of December 1964) is aimed at the departure that most directly threatened D1, the deepest and most temporally stable frame in the entire corpus; by contrast, W. D. Muhammad's structurally similar 1975 departure from D1 does not, in the sampled documents, generate any comparable delegitimization response *directed at him* — because he held, rather than lacked, institutional authority over the movement's press and pulpit at the moment of the change. This suggests institutional control over the means of framing, not the substance of the frame change itself, may be a generative condition determining whether frame change produces schism-with-delegitimization or institutionally-absorbed reform. This is a retroductive hypothesis about a possible mechanism; the corpus does not contain direct evidence of decision-making by movement authorities about press control as a strategic matter.

3. **State surveillance (documented independently in the 1955 and 1957 FBI files, discussed below) constructed and disseminated its own competing diagnostic frame — organizational classification as a "Cult" exhibiting "Racial Hatred" and "Advocacy of Violence," filed under a table-of-contents heading of "Potential Threat to Internal Security"** — operating entirely independently of, and in direct tension with, the movement's own diagnostic self-understanding. This is directly evidenced by the FBI file's own table of contents and body text (Section headings: "Racial Hatred," "Advocacy of Violence," "Subversive Tendencies," "Potential Threat to Internal Security," 1955). It is offered here as evidence of a *third* framing actor beyond the two (Elijah Muhammad's NOI and Malcolm X's post-1964 organizations) analyzed above in Parts I–VII — a state security frame that diagnosed the movement's very existence, independent of its internal doctrinal or leadership disputes, as the problem to be solved. The corpus does not permit confident inference about the causal effect of this state framing on the movement's internal dynamics (e.g., whether surveillance pressure contributed to internal loyalty-policing); this is flagged explicitly as a boundary the sampled evidence cannot cross.

# PART IX: FINAL SYNTHESIS

## Diagnostic Architecture
Three diagnostic frames operate across the corpus: a cosmological/racial-essentialist frame (D1) that is temporally dominant and load-bearing under Elijah Muhammad's leadership (1957–1974) but is independently abandoned by two successive figures (Malcolm X, 1964; W. D. Muhammad, 1975) at moments of direct, documented contact with the wider international Muslim world; a political-institutional frame (D2) present throughout but subordinated under Elijah Muhammad and foregrounded by Malcolm X after March 1964; and a pedagogical/false-consciousness frame (D3) that is the corpus's most continuous diagnostic thread, persisting in structurally parallel form on both sides of every documented schism.

## Prognostic Architecture
Four prognostic frames are documented as sequential replacements rather than compatible options: divine separation (P1, Elijah Muhammad, stable 1957–1974); electoral/community self-determination (P2, Malcolm X, March–April 1964 only); Pan-African human-rights internationalization (P3, Malcolm X, April–1965); and reintegrative universalization (P4, W. D. Muhammad, from February 1975). Each transition between prognoses is directly evidenced by dated documents; none is presented in the corpus as a synthesis of its predecessor.

## Motivational Architecture
Prophetic/eschatological urgency (M1) and loyalty/hypocrisy discourse (M2) jointly sustain Elijah Muhammad's frame package and are weaponized with maximal intensity at the exact moment (late 1964) that the package's central diagnostic claim (D1) is most directly and publicly contradicted by a former senior figure. Universal brotherhood (M3) emerges independently in 1964 and 1975 as the motivational corollary of D1's abandonment, in both cases tied to direct experience of the international Muslim world rather than to domestic political calculation alone.

## Leadership and Contestation
The corpus provides direct, dated, first-person evidence of two structurally similar frame-transformation events (1964, 1975) with two structurally dissimilar organizational outcomes (schism with lethal-register delegitimization discourse; absorbed institutional reform). The single best-supported explanatory distinction the corpus permits is institutional control over the means of publication and doctrinal authority at the moment of change — a factor present for W. D. Muhammad and absent for Malcolm X — though this is identified here as the strongest available inference, not a fully demonstrated causal finding.

## Overall Transformation
Read across its full twenty-two-year span, the corpus documents a movement whose diagnostic core (D1) proved simultaneously durable enough to survive unmodified for at least seventeen consecutive years of one man's leadership and fragile enough to be independently abandoned, within months of direct contrary experience, by two of the three other major figures examined here (Malcolm X; W. D. Muhammad) — with the fourth figure examined (Louis X/Farrakhan, in his one sampled 1964 document) serving not as an independent frame-generator but as the corpus's clearest instance of frame *defense* through personal delegitimization of a dissenter, a role that this analysis does not extend into his later, post-1977 restorationist career, which lies outside the evidentiary window and is not characterized here.

# EVIDENTIARY RULES APPLIED

This report has attempted to observe the following standards throughout:

1. **Evidence before interpretation:** direct quotations are provided for every frame claim; interpretive claims are explicitly marked as such.
2. **No unsupported mechanisms:** every "Real Level (Retroduction)" section is explicitly flagged as inference, not established fact, and the specific evidentiary limits of each inference are stated.
3. **Mechanism rather than event:** where possible, the analysis identifies recurring structural relationships (e.g., diagnosis-first sequencing in frame transformation; institutional control as a variable in schism-vs-absorption outcomes) rather than resting on narration of single events alone.
4. **No retrospective contamination:** Louis Farrakhan's post-1977 speeches are excluded from the analytical core and mentioned only as an explicitly bounded epilogue; W. D. Muhammad's 1975 material, though at the edge of the window, is included because it falls within 1952–1977 and is directly relevant to the succession this analysis examines.
5. **No doctrinal summary presented as neutral fact:** all doctrinal claims (e.g., Yacub's history, the devil-by-nature teaching) are presented explicitly as the movement's or an individual's *frame*, not endorsed or restated as fact by this report.
6. **Analytical distinctions preserved:** diagnostic, prognostic, and motivational frames are kept separate throughout, including where a single document (e.g., Elijah Muhammad's September 1964 response) performs more than one function.
7. **Frequency is not sufficient:** frame importance ratings are grounded in demonstrated organizational or motivational *function* (e.g., licensing a specific prognosis, sustaining a specific institutional pattern), not merely in word-count or repetition.
8. **Leadership differences demonstrated, not asserted:** every claimed difference between Elijah Muhammad, Malcolm X, Louis X/Farrakhan, and W. D. Muhammad is grounded in a specific dated document with direct quotation.
9. **Negative evidence handled carefully:** the "worthy of death" language and its proximity to Malcolm X's assassination are reported as documented textual fact without asserting an unsupported causal link to the assassination itself.
10. **Quotations:** provided directly from the sampled corpus throughout, with source document and date.
11. **Analytical standard:** this report aims to meet the standard of a rigorous, evidence-bounded sociological frame analysis; where the corpus's limits (partial OCR quality on some newspaper scans, a curated rather than exhaustive sample of 34 documents out of 591 available files) constrain confidence, this is stated rather than papered over.
