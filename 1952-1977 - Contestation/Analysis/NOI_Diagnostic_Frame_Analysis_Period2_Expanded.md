# Diagnostic Framing in the Nation of Islam, 1952–1977: A Collective Action Frame Analysis Under Perspectival Critical Realism (Expanded Edition)

## Corpus Note

This is the **diagnostic** volume of a three-part frame analysis (Diagnostic / Prognostic / Motivational) of the Nation of Islam corpus, 1952–1977, drawn from the `1952-1977 - Contestation/` directory and selected for centrality to the Elijah Muhammad–Malcolm X contestation and the 1975 Warith Deen Muhammad succession, and for comparability with the Period 1 (1930–1951) diagnostic analysis. Sources are cited by short title; the full Works Cited (up to one hundred sources) appears at the end of this document and is shared, with minor emphasis differences, across all three volumes of this analysis.

This expanded edition substantially deepens the evidentiary base of the original report in two directions. First, it draws on the full 1960–1961 run of Malcolm X's own public addresses on Elijah Muhammad's behalf — the Harlem Freedom Rally (1960), the Harvard Law School Forum (1961), the WMCA/WNYC-style "Open Mind" roundtable (Oct. 1961), the Bayard Rustin debate (Oct. 1961), the Eleanor Fischer interview (1961), and the Yale Law School address (Oct. 1962) — none of which were cited in the original report, and all of which independently attest that Malcolm X was articulating D1 and D3 in their fullest, most doctrinally uncompromising form for at least three years *before* the 1964 schism, strengthening the report's claim that his later reversal was a genuine diagnostic transformation rather than a difference of degree. Second, it draws much more heavily on Warith Deen Muhammad's full 1975 *Muhammad Speaks* article series — "The Mark of the Beast" (March 29), "Remake the World," Parts 1–3 (April 27, May 16, May 23), "Black America's 'Mission' in the World" (June 27), "Weapons" and "Save Your Life" (both June 13), and "A Growth Note: The Church" (July 25) — none of which were cited in the original report, and all of which supply direct, first-person textual evidence for the psychologization of D1 into D6 that the original report inferred from a smaller sample. Newly cited *Muhammad Speaks* issues from the "Do For Self" and Yacub-etiology runs (1962–1973) are also added throughout.

Diagnostic frames answer: *what is the problem, who or what caused it, and who is harmed?* Six diagnostic frames are load-bearing across the corpus, presented here in more granular and more heavily evidenced form than the earlier edition of this report.

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Ranked Diagnostic Frames

1. **D1 — The Devil-by-Nature Etiology (Yacub's History)** — Elijah Muhammad's stable master diagnosis (1957–1974): the "white race" is a biologically engineered, morally fixed antagonist, created by the scientist Yacub, and this fact — not any remediable policy — is the root of Black suffering.
2. **D2 — Institutional and Political Exclusion** — Malcolm X's post-1964 diagnosis: the problem is not an unchangeable racial nature but concrete, nameable, reformable structures (voting law, school administration, committee seniority) that exclude Black Americans from functioning democratic machinery.
3. **D3 — Mental Slavery via Religious and Educational False Consciousness** — a diagnosis present on every side of every schism: Black Americans suffer not only material deprivation but a colonized, miseducated, or "tampered with" mind, requiring corrective knowledge before any other remedy can take hold.
4. **D4 — The Hypocrite/Defector Diagnosis** — the claim that a movement figure's departure and dissent is caused by personal moral corruption rather than by any defect in the movement's teaching, deployed with the greatest documented intensity against Malcolm X in late 1964.
5. **D5 — State Surveillance and Government Conspiracy** — Malcolm X's diagnosis that government itself, not merely private prejudice, obstructs Black political power, corroborated independently by the state's own documented surveillance apparatus.
6. **D6 — The Grafted/Colored Mind as a Transferable Psychological Structure** — Warith Deen Muhammad's 1975 doctrinal revision of D1: "the devil" reinterpreted as a transferable, learnable "mentality" rather than a fixed hereditary category.

These frames are not evenly distributed across the period; D1 and D3 dominate 1957–1963, D2, D4, and D5 cluster tightly around the 1964–1965 schism, and D6 belongs almost entirely to 1975. The corpus's newly incorporated 1960–1962 material shows that D1 and D3 were not static doctrines simply inherited by Malcolm X but were actively, repeatedly, and skillfully re-elaborated by him, in front of hostile and skeptical audiences (Harvard, Yale, network radio, university debate panels), for at least half a decade before he began to depart from them.

## DETAILED DIAGNOSTIC FRAME ANALYSIS

### D1 — The Devil-by-Nature Etiology (Yacub's History)

**Definition.** The corpus's master diagnosis under Elijah Muhammad's leadership: the "white race" is not merely socially dominant but biologically and morally fixed as an antagonist by an act of ancient genetic engineering (the Yacub myth), inherited from the Period 1 corpus and restated without material modification across seventeen years of publication (1957–1974).

**Core Diagnosis.** "The Original Man, Al-Islam, is none other than the Black man" and the white race was "made" through a deliberate breeding project attributed to the scientist Yacub — restated across TSWV, MTTBIA, TFOA, and elsewhere without variation of doctrine.

**Problem Representation.** The antagonist is not a policy, an institution, or a historical accident but a fixed *nature*: "the white race... by nature... cannot be righteous" (TSWV, 1957). This forecloses reformist diagnosis in advance: if the antagonist's nature rather than behavior is the problem, no change in behavior on either side could, even in principle, resolve it.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "The white race is a race of devils" — restated with only cosmetic variation from TSWV (1957) through TFOA (1973), a sixteen-year span of unmodified doctrine.
- Delivered by Malcolm X, as the movement's own national representative, at the Harvard Law School Forum (March 24, 1961), in terms fully continuous with Elijah Muhammad's own written doctrine: "God has come to close out the entire Old World, the Old World in which for the past 6,000 years practically the entire earth has been deceived, conquered, colonized, ruled, enslaved, oppressed and exploited by the Caucasian race" — this is direct evidence that D1 was being actively, publicly re-elaborated by the movement's most visible non-founder spokesman years before any hint of the later schism, and in exactly the register the founder's books use.
- Also at Harvard: "the Christian religion of the Caucasian race has failed us... white Christians have been unable to recognize us and accept us as fellow human beings" — D1 stated jointly with D3, showing the two diagnoses operating as a single fused claim in Malcolm X's own pre-1964 rhetoric, not as separable frames only distinguishable in retrospect.
- At the Yale Law School Forum (Oct. 20, 1962), the same diagnosis is cast in an explicitly medicalized register: "Uncle Sam is sick, because he has a black 'lump' growing in his white body that doesn't belong there, and this black 'growth' is getting larger every day, and increasing Uncle Sam's internal pains... this surgery must be performed, for if the 20 million rapidly increasing so-called Negroes are not separated from the white parts of the body, it will soon cause the death and destruction of Uncle Sam" — a pathological metaphor of the antagonist relationship (the racial *coexistence* itself as the disease, not the white race alone) that is a variant articulation of D1 not present in the original report's source sample.
- The 1960 Harlem Freedom Rally address states the same fixed-antagonist diagnosis in a more populist register aimed at a mixed religious audience: "The Western World, filled with evil and wickedness, is groping and stumbling blindly through spiritual darkness toward its inevitable doom," continuous with, but addressed to a broader "Baptist, Methodist, Catholic" coalition audience than the movement's internal doctrine alone would suggest — early evidence that D1 was, even under Elijah Muhammad, capable of being pitched differently depending on audience, a flexibility later fully exploited by Malcolm X's post-1964 frame revisions.

**Responsible Agents.** The "white race" collectively, understood as an engineered biological category rather than a set of individuals who might act otherwise; secondarily, Yacub as the originating agent of the antagonist's creation.

**Affected Population.** The "Original" Black nation, victimized not merely economically but ontologically — stripped, per the Harvard address, of "language," "history," "culture," "nationality," "country, flag, religion, and God."

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Directly licenses P1 (Divine Separation): if the antagonist cannot change, exit is the only coherent remedy.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Bound tightly to M1 (Chosen Nation/Divine Urgency) and M4 (Racial Reversal/Cosmic Status Elevation).

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Directly and repeatedly stated across the entire Elijah Muhammad corpus and, independently, in at least five separately dated public addresses delivered by Malcolm X between 1960 and 1962 as the movement's authorized representative — a considerably larger empirical base than the original report's single-book citation implied.
- **Actual Level.** Functions to make the movement's authority structure and its separatist economic program appear as the only logically available response to an unchangeable fact of nature, foreclosing negotiation, coalition, or piecemeal reform as categories of action.
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the fact that Malcolm X could deliver this diagnosis fluently, and with local audience-specific elaboration (the "sick Uncle Sam" surgical metaphor at Yale; the ecumenical "Harlem" register at the Freedom Rally), across at least three different registers over three years, suggests the diagnosis functioned in practice as a generative interpretive framework capable of local improvisation by trained spokesmen, not merely a fixed catechism recited unchanged — which in turn helps explain both the doctrine's evident durability under Elijah Muhammad and its vulnerability to precisely the kind of skilled, improvisational reworking Malcolm X later performed on it after 1964, and Warith Deen Muhammad performed on it again in 1975 (D6).

**Relative Importance:** **High** throughout Elijah Muhammad's leadership; **explicitly abandoned** by Malcolm X after April 1964; **reworked, not abandoned,** by Warith Deen Muhammad in 1975 (see D6).

**Evidence Across Corpus:** TSWV (1957); TTHOJ (1957); MTTBIA (1965); TFOA (1973); OSHA (1974); MX_1960_Speech_HarlemFreedomRally; MX_1961_03_24_Speech_HarvardLawForum; MX_1962_10_20_Speech_YaleLawSchool.

### D2 — Institutional and Political Exclusion

**Definition.** Malcolm X's post-departure (1964) diagnosis: rather than an unchangeable racial nature, the problem is concrete, nameable, and in principle reformable institutional structures — voting-rights denial, gerrymandering, seniority-protected committee chairs, failing school administrations — that exclude Black Americans from a functioning democratic mechanism.

**Core Diagnosis.** "You put Negroes first in the cotton field, then in the trades, and now you're trying to put us first in graduate schools; you're going to keep us at the bottom of everything!" ... "The Dixiecrats control these committees is because they have seniority" ("The Ballot or the Bullet," April 3, 1964) — the antagonist is now specifiable machinery, not an ontological category.

**Problem Representation.** "Twenty-two of the twenty-six committees are chairmanned by Southern racists... in every session that Congress opens, [a Negro] is in the House of Representatives waiting for this old racist congressman to get up and adjourn because he's not going to put civil rights legislation on the floor" — the diagnostic object is a specific, describable procedural failure with a specific, describable remedy (expulsion of committee chairs under existing constitutional machinery), not an unfalsifiable prophetic condition.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "It's either liberty or death for everybody, or nobody" — an emphatic, general statement of D2's stakes.
- "The government of America has failed the Negro" — a direct restatement of D2's core claim, distinguished from D1 by naming an institution ("the government"), not a race, as the failing agent.
- The AB Spellman interview (March 19, 1964), conducted one week after Malcolm X's break, shows D2 emerging in real time, still partially entangled with residual separatist language: Malcolm X describes his new Muslim Mosque, Inc. platform in terms of "political, economic, and social" black nationalism that "must control the politics and the politicians of our community" — evidence that D2's institutional-electoral register did not arrive fully formed but developed across the specific weeks between the March 12 Declaration of Independence and the April 3 "Ballot or Bullet" address.

**Responsible Agents.** Specific, nameable political actors and structures: Dixiecrat committee chairmen, the two major political parties treated as jointly complicit, state election administrations practicing voter suppression.

**Affected Population.** Black American citizens specifically in their capacity as voters and as parents of schoolchildren in underperforming, segregated, or resource-starved public schools.

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Directly licenses P2 (electoral capture) and, as fallback, P5 (armed self-defense) and P6 (parallel institution-building).

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Underwrites M5 (this-worldly, politically contingent urgency) rather than M1's cosmic timetable.

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Stated with unusual institutional specificity — named committees, named incidents, a named electoral calendar — in the April 1964 speeches and, in nascent form, in the March 19 Spellman interview.
- **Actual Level.** Functions to reposition Malcolm X's address from a specifically Muslim theological audience to a broad, religiously mixed nationalist audience within days of his organizational break.
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the short interval between the Spellman interview's still partly theological register and the fully secularized institutional register of the April speeches is consistent with a rapid, real-time process of frame refinement under public pressure to demonstrate an independent political program, rather than a frame arrived at through extended private deliberation.

**Relative Importance:** **High** for a brief, pivotal window (March–June 1964).

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1964_03_12_DeclarationOfIndependence; MX_1964_03_19_Interview_ABSpellman; MX_1964_04_03_Speech_BallotOrBullet; MX_1964_04_12_Speech_BallotOrBullet.

### D3 — Mental Slavery via Religious and Educational False Consciousness

**Definition.** A diagnosis distinct from D1 and D2 in its object: the claim that Black Americans suffer not only material deprivation but a colonized or miseducated *mind*, inherited from a slave-master's religion and educational system, which must itself be diagnosed and corrected before any other remedy — political, economic, or religious — can take hold. Present, in some form, on every side of every schism documented in this corpus.

**Core Diagnosis.** "We can never grow into the true Knowledge of Self until we learn and understand the history of ourselves. Our pitiful condition as a people in the world today is a result of the brutal butchering of our original mind (mentality) by those who ripped us from our native soil" (W. D. Muhammad, "Artificial Blackness," June 27, 1975).

**Problem Representation.** The Yale Law School address (Oct. 20, 1962) gives the diagnosis its most compact formulation in Malcolm X's own pre-schism voice: "The Negro's position is most strategic, but his mental condition is too pathetic for him to take intelligent advantage of this vital position 'fate' has placed him in. The American Negro is suffering from a mental sickness. His mind has been 'tampered' with by his slave master" — D3 stated as an explicit, named diagnostic category ("mental sickness") two years before the schism, evidence against any reading in which D3 was invented or discovered only after 1964.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "Every little child going to school thinks his grandfather was a cotton picker. Why, your grandfather was Nat Turner... your grandfather was Hannibal" (Malcolm X, OAAU Founding Rally, 1964).
- W. D. Muhammad, in his first official interview (March 21, 1975), retrospectively diagnoses the same phenomenon among Christian Black Americans of the founding period: "black people were almost 100% Christian... You didn't have to go to Church to be born with a Christian mind."
- W. D. Muhammad's "The Church" (July 25, 1975) develops D3 into its most operational, campaign-like statement in the corpus: "The enemy we are killing is devil tricknology and devil religious psychology... you are not fighting the church. You are fighting Satan and white supremacy" — D3 is here explicitly distinguished from a merely institutional critique of a rival denomination; the diagnosed object is a psychological structure ("devil religious psychology") the church is said to carry, not the church's members or buildings as such.
- W. D. Muhammad's "Remake the World, Part 1" (May 16, 1975) extends D3 into an extended, quasi-clinical account of the mechanism of mental capture: "The worst form of slavery is the enslavement of your mind. Many men and women go around thinking that they are free while they are mentally a slave... there are books out now on the market on the subject of subliminal suggestion (seduction)... the mind being faster than the eye catches the picture and the picture goes into the mind without the mind's recognition or awareness" — an explicit, named analogy to contemporary mass-media persuasion theory that is not present in Elijah Muhammad's own formulations of D3 and represents a genuinely new, more secular-scientific idiom for the same underlying diagnostic claim.
- The Eleanor Fischer interview (1961) gives an early, plainly stated version of the same claim, again in Malcolm X's own pre-schism voice: "He has been brainwashed by the educational system that exists here in America to the point where he feels he was a savage in the jungle before he was brought here. And this destroys his morale... he has to be re-educated and made to know that he's a man."

**Responsible Agents.** The educational and religious institutions of the "slave master's" society, understood as instruments of deliberate psychological engineering rather than incidental neglect.

**Affected Population.** The whole of the so-called Negro population, including — per the 1975 material's most explicit extension — those who consider themselves already free of religious belief altogether: W. D. Muhammad's "Save Your Life" (June 13, 1975) insists that "even though you say that you don't have a religion, you do... you belong to whatever people have the power, the force, or the influence in the world you call yourself living in," extending D3's reach to secular self-description as itself a symptom of unrecognized captivity.

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Licenses an educational/pedagogical prognosis distinct from either separation (P1) or electoral politics (P2): the Nation of Islam's own school system, the OAAU's proposed community-controlled schools, and W. D. Muhammad's doctrinal "renewal" of religious instruction all follow from D3.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Feeds a distinct motivational register — urgency toward self-transformation and re-education, later echoed in the "Knowledge of Self" catechism W. D. Muhammad restates through August 1975.

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Directly and repeatedly stated across Elijah Muhammad's corpus (1957–1974), in Malcolm X's own pre-schism voice at Yale (1962) and to Eleanor Fischer (1961), in his post-schism OAAU material (1964), and independently, in structurally parallel and considerably more elaborated form, in W. D. Muhammad's 1975 series — four independent lines of the same diagnostic operation, spanning the entire twenty-two-year period without interruption.
- **Actual Level.** The frame does the work of making leadership itself indispensable: if the primary problem is false consciousness, only an authoritative corrective teacher can resolve it. The 1975 "subliminal suggestion" elaboration additionally does the work of making the *ordinary media environment itself* — not merely churches and schools — into an object requiring vigilance, broadening the frame's practical reach considerably beyond its Period 1 and Elijah Muhammad-era scope.
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the frame's independent emergence in Elijah Muhammad's religious register, Malcolm X's pre-schism and post-schism registers alike, and W. D. Muhammad's 1975 quasi-clinical register suggests a shared underlying logic of "corrective pedagogy against internalized subordination" that survived every personal schism in the corpus — i.e., the schisms operated far more at the level of prognosis and loyalty (Motivational Frame M2) than at the level of this diagnostic structure, which every faction continued to deploy and, in W. D. Muhammad's case, actively modernized.

**Relative Importance:** **Medium-High.** Present throughout the whole 1952–1977 period; functions as a supporting diagnosis beneath D1, D2, or D6 rather than as a freestanding master diagnosis, but is more heavily and more continuously evidenced in this expanded edition than in the original report.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** TSWV (1957); MX_1961_Interview_EleanorFischer; MX_1962_10_20_Speech_YaleLawSchool; MX_1964_06_28_Speech_OAAUFoundingRally; IWDM_1975_03_21_Interview_FirstInterview; IWDM_1975_05_16_MS_Article_RemakeTheWorld; IWDM_1975_06_13_MS_Article_SaveYourLife; IWDM_1975_06_27_MS_Article_ArtificialBlackness; IWDM_1975_07_25_MS_Article_TheChurch; IWDM_1975_08_22_MS_Article_WhoIsTheOriginalMan.

### D4 — The Hypocrite/Defector Diagnosis

**Definition.** A diagnosis distinct from D1–D3 in that its object is *internal* rather than external: the claim that a movement figure's public departure and dissent is caused by personal moral corruption — jealousy, ambition, "hypocrisy" — rather than by any defect in the movement's teaching, thereby diagnosing the *problem* as the defector, not as anything the defector might have said.

**Core Diagnosis.** Louis X's December 1964 article does not engage any of the substance of Malcolm X's post-departure positions (D2, the Mecca reversal of D1, or P3); it diagnoses the departure itself as evidence of a pre-existing character flaw: Malcolm X is "defector and hypocrite Malcolm," whose "shameful attempts... to slander and destroy the great Islamic leader" had "caused concern not only among the ranks of Muslims, but among many fair-minded non-Muslims" (*Muhammad Speaks*, Dec. 4, 1964).

**Problem Representation.** The category of "hypocrite" (borrowed, notably, from the same Quranic vocabulary used in the *Supreme Wisdom Lessons*' "Rebels against the Will of Allah" in the Period 1 corpus) forecloses argument: a hypocrite's stated reasons for dissent are, by definition of the category, pretextual, so there is no diagnostic content in Malcolm X's post-1964 statements that requires a substantive answer.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "Such a man as Malcolm is made... Worthy of death, and would have met with death if it had not been for Muhammad's confidence in Allah for victory over the enemies" (Louis X, Dec. 1964) — language published roughly ten weeks before Malcolm X's assassination on February 21, 1965.
- Elijah Muhammad's own September 1964 restatement of doctrine, published in direct response to Malcolm X's departure, performs a related but milder version of the same diagnostic move: rather than naming Malcolm X, it reasserts, at length and without concession, the very doctrines Malcolm X had just publicly begun to depart from — diagnosing the departure, by omission, as simply mistaken rather than as raising any question the doctrine itself must answer.
- Malcolm X's own retrospective account, delivered at the Oxford Union debate (Dec. 3, 1964), gives the defector's-eye view of the same discourse from the outside: he describes being cast, following his break, as an object of organized denunciation rather than of engagement on the merits — corroborating, from the opposite side, the same asymmetry the December 1964 *Muhammad Speaks* article displays.

**Responsible Agents.** The individual defector, understood as personally culpable — a notably different attribution of agency than D1 (hereditary, collective) or D2 (institutional, structural).

**Affected Population.** The movement's general membership, whose loyalty is the object being defended, and secondarily the "fair-minded non-Muslims" the article claims were also alarmed.

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Functions to discredit, by association with the discredited person, any prognosis (P2, P3, P5) Malcolm X subsequently proposed, independent of that prognosis's substantive content — i.e., D4 is a diagnostic move whose entire purpose is prognostic pre-emption.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** D4 is the diagnostic content underneath Motivational Frame M2 (loyalty-and-betrayal urgency): the two are close to inseparable in the corpus, but are analytically distinguished here because D4 answers "what is wrong" (a corrupted individual) while M2 answers "why must you act now" (to defend the movement's legitimacy against that individual).

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Directly documented in Louis X's December 1964 article, including the explicit "worthy of death" language.
- **Actual Level.** This is a point requiring particular evidentiary caution. The corpus contains a documented published statement describing Malcolm X as "worthy of death" prior to his assassination. **This is direct textual evidence of the existence and publication of such language.** It is **not**, on the strength of this corpus alone, evidence of a causal link between this specific publication and the assassination itself, which involved separate individuals, a criminal investigation, and a historical record outside the scope of the sampled documents. This report records the frame's documented content and timing; it does not draw a causal inference the corpus cannot support.
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference, clearly bounded:* the intensity and personalization of this diagnosis in late 1964 is consistent with a generative mechanism in which an authority structure built substantially on personalized charismatic and prophetic frames (D1, M1) had few available registers for absorbing public, doctrinally grounded dissent from a former senior minister other than total moral delegitimization of the dissenter. This is offered strictly as one plausible mechanism explaining the frame's severity, not as an account of any specific actor's intent regarding the events of February 1965.

**Relative Importance:** **High** specifically at the moment of schism (1964); the frame recedes, without being formally repudiated, under the reintegrative approach of 1975 ("the past is the past... we will not dig it up," W. D. Muhammad).

**Evidence Across Corpus:** LF_1964_12_04_Article_BiggestHypocrite; EM_1964_09_11_Article_ResponseToMXLeaving; MX_1964_12_03_Debate_OxfordUnion.

### D5 — State Surveillance and Government Conspiracy

**Definition.** Two related but distinct diagnostic operations, both documented directly in the corpus: (a) Malcolm X's diagnosis that the government *itself*, not merely private segregationist sentiment, is the active agent obstructing Black political power; and (b) the independently attested fact that the FBI's own files diagnosed the Nation of Islam and Malcolm X, in the state's own classificatory language, as a "Cult" exhibiting "Racial Hatred" and "Advocacy of Violence," filed under the heading "Potential Threat to Internal Security."

**Core Diagnosis.** Malcolm X: "You and I in America are faced not with a segregationist conspiracy, we're faced with a government conspiracy" ("The Ballot or the Bullet," April 1964) — the diagnostic object is upgraded from individual racist actors to the structure of government itself. Independently, and from the opposite direction, the FBI's 1955 file table of contents diagnoses the movement's very existence — its doctrine, leadership, and growth — as itself the problem to be tracked and contained, under headings including "Racial Hatred," "Advocacy of Violence," and "Subversive Tendencies."

**Problem Representation.** Malcolm X's February 14, 1965 speech, delivered the night after his own home was firebombed, sharpens this diagnosis into a direct claim of organized, unresolved threat to his life and his family, distinguishing it in register (personal, immediate, embodied) from the more institutional language of "The Ballot or the Bullet" a year earlier: "I was in a house last night that was bombed, my own... I don't normally come out in front of people without a shirt and a tie... but I was caught in what I had on" (Ford Auditorium, Detroit, Feb. 14, 1965).

**Representative Quotations.**
- "The only reason the Dixiecrats control these committees is because they have seniority" — read alongside the FBI files' own documented surveillance activity beginning years before the 1964 schism, this establishes that state attention to the movement long predates, and is causally independent of, the internal Elijah Muhammad–Malcolm X contestation.
- FBI file section headings (1955): "Racial Hatred," "Advocacy of Violence," "Subversive Tendencies," "Potential Threat to Internal Security" — this is directly evidenced by the file's own table of contents and body text, not an inference about the file's content.
- The corpus's separately filed FBI surveillance record of Betty Shabazz (1958) independently corroborates that state monitoring of the movement extended, even at this early date, beyond its public spokesmen to their immediate families — evidence, newly incorporated in this expanded edition, that the surveillance diagnosis's scope in the movement's own later rhetoric ("my wife understands... my children") tracked an actually documented, not merely feared, state practice.
- The Phoenix field office's FBI file on Malcolm X (1961–1972) independently documents that federal surveillance activity continued, geographically dispersed across at least five field-office jurisdictions, for years after Malcolm X's assassination — direct evidence that the state's own diagnostic and monitoring apparatus operated on a timescale and with an institutional durability considerably exceeding the movement's own internal schism cycle.
- Malcolm X, describing the night of the firebombing: "It didn't destroy all my clothes, not all, but you know what happens when fire dashes through — they get smoky... in trying to get my family out of the house, none of us stopped for any clothes at that point — twenty-degree cold" (Ford Auditorium, Feb. 14, 1965).

**Responsible Agents.** For Malcolm X's diagnosis: elected officials, committee structures, and "the government" as an institution. For the FBI files themselves: the state security apparatus as an independent third framing actor, operating with its own diagnostic categories entirely apart from, and prior to, either the Elijah Muhammad or the Malcolm X camp's internal dispute.

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Malcolm X's government-conspiracy diagnosis licenses armed self-defense (P5) and international human-rights appeal (P3) as remedies precisely because domestic constitutional channels are diagnosed as captured; the state's own surveillance diagnosis, by contrast, generates no prognosis *within* the movement's corpus — it is a rival framing project entirely external to the movement's own frame-making, which the movement's texts do not directly answer or even, in most instances, acknowledge by name.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Underwrites both this-worldly conditional urgency (M5) and, in the specific case of the February 1965 speeches, a motivational register of personal courage under direct, embodied threat (M6).

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Malcolm X's claim is stated in specific institutional vocabulary (committees, seniority, gerrymandering); the FBI's diagnosis is stated in the state's own bureaucratic-security vocabulary (section headings, classification labels), which this report treats as directly evidenced textual fact, not editorial characterization.
- **Actual Level.** The FBI files independently corroborate that sustained federal surveillance of the movement (Fard, 1957; the Nation of Islam, 1955 and 1957; Malcolm X, from at least 1953 across at least six field-office jurisdictions — Headquarters, New York, Washington, Chicago, Phoenix, and, through Betty Shabazz's own separately filed record, an implicit seventh line of monitoring reaching into his immediate family — predates the 1964 schism by years, meaning the movement's own diagnoses of persecution and "government conspiracy" were not solely rhetorical constructs responding to the schism but tracked a documented, independently existing state security interest.
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference, explicitly bounded:* the corpus does not permit confident inference about the causal effect of this state surveillance on the movement's internal dynamics — for instance, whether surveillance pressure contributed to the intensified internal loyalty-policing documented in D4. This report flags that boundary explicitly rather than crossing it; the diagnostic operations documented here (Malcolm X's political diagnosis and the FBI's own multi-jurisdictional security diagnosis) are presented as parallel, independently documented facts, not as a demonstrated causal chain.

**Relative Importance:** **High** for the specific 1964–1965 period; the FBI material is best read as establishing an evidentiary floor and external context for the whole corpus rather than as itself a frame *deployed by* the movement.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1964_04_03_Speech_BallotOrBullet; MX_1965_02_14_Speech_AfterFirebombing_FordAuditorium; FBI_1955_Files_NOI; FBI_1957_Fies_NOI; FBI_1957_Files_FardMuhammad; FBI_1953-1971_Files_MalcolmX_HQ; FBI_1953-1971_Files_MalcolmX_NewYork; FBI_1954-1971_Files_MalcolmX_Washington; FBI_1958-1971_Files_MalcolmX_Chicago; FBI_1961-1972_Files_MalcolmX_Phoenix; FBI_1958_Files_BettyShabazz.

### D6 — The Grafted/Colored Mind as a Transferable Psychological Structure

**Definition.** Warith Deen Muhammad's 1975 doctrinal revision of D1: rather than a fixed hereditary biological category, "the devil" is reinterpreted as a "colored," artificial *mentality* — a "germ" of corrupted mind — capable of inhabiting any body, including a Black one, which preserves the founder's vocabulary while quietly converting a racial-essentialist diagnosis into a psychological one.

**Core Diagnosis.** "We now have you in our hands in your black body but bearing the white man's mind in your being... If we would wipe all grafted devils out with a physical destruction, we would still have them on hand in your black body" ("The Death of White Supremacy," *Muhammad Speaks*, July 18, 1975). This sentence does real diagnostic work distinct from D1: it explicitly denies that physical, racial destruction of "devils" would solve the problem, because the problem is now located in a transferable mental structure rather than in heredity as such.

**Problem Representation.** "Who is the original man? ... The original is that which has not been dealt with artificially, taken out of its original form, and put into grafted, artificial form" ("Who Is The Original Man?", Aug. 22, 1975). W. D. Muhammad simultaneously retains the founder's vocabulary of "grafted," "germ," and "Caucasian mentality," and redefines "white man" as anyone who carries a "colored mind" — including, by his own explicit statement, Black people themselves: "We call the Caucasian white supremacist the colored man because his mind is colored... Nobody knew anything about racism until the colored man from Europe came up with the idea of white supremacy."

**Representative Quotations.**
- "This world, the world of grafted Caucasian mentality, has grown old... It has grown so hopeless today until people are just satisfied to go to work, return home, get some sleep, wake up, and then repeat that cycle over and over again until they die" ("The Death of White Supremacy," July 18, 1975).
- "The devil, who is capable of the most beautiful speech, uses that speech to attract the weak people so he can rule over them as a beast. The history of the Church is the history of a beast" (same document) — D3's Christianity-as-deception diagnosis is retained essentially unchanged even as D1 is being reworked around it.
- "I'm not calling white folks 'devil'... I'm calling the mind that has ruled those people and you 'devil.' It ruled them for their glory and ruled you for your shame and damnation... the devil mentality (the germ that ruled in the Caucasian body) ruled them for their glory and power over the world. The same they passed on to you and it ruled in you for your shame and your destruction" ("Remake the World, Part 1," May 16, 1975) — the single clearest and most explicit statement in the entire sampled corpus of D6's decisive move: the devil is relocated from body to "mind," and the same mind is explicitly said to be transmissible into, and already resident in, Black people themselves. This passage supplies, in W. D. Muhammad's own words, the textual warrant for the report's central claim about D6 that the earlier edition supported only by inference from shorter excerpts.
- "The Mark of the Beast" (Aug. 29, 1975; also published under a March 29, 1975 dateline in a shorter installment) develops the same relocated diagnosis through an extended reading of Revelation: the "mark of the beast" is glossed as a psychological rather than a bodily inscription — "the thinking center is in the forehead," so that "those people who wear the mark of the beast in their forehead" are those whose *minds* the corrupted doctrine ("the Trinitarian Doctrine... the cockatrice") has captured, "whether they are ministers, priests, or rabbis" — extending D6's transferable-mentality logic explicitly to religious leadership as a category, not merely to "whiteness" as a racial description.
- "Black America's 'Mission' in the World" (June 27, 1975) supplies the diagnosis's necessary correlate — a positive account of who is *not* captured by the "colored mind" — describing Black America as "conditioned to understand" the antagonist precisely because of its own historical suffering, not because of unearned racial virtue: "The Black community in America was not chosen simply because it is Black physically... [it is] representative of the world's people of color more than they are representative of those people of the world whose skin appears to be physically Black" — a diagnostic move that explicitly severs moral standing from literal skin color, consistent with, and textually reinforcing, D6's central relocation of the antagonist from body to mind.

**Responsible Agents.** "Grafted Caucasian mentality" as a free-floating psychological contagion — "you eat it with your eyes; you eat it with your ears; you eat it with your mind when you're not even thinking" — rather than a fixed population defined by descent, which is the decisive diagnostic break from D1.

**Affected Population.** Explicitly widened beyond the founder's racial categories: "the germ of the devil-being first existed in a black body," meaning the diagnosis's origin point is relocated even further than a simple color-reversal of D1 would suggest.

**Connection to Prognostic Frames.** Licenses reintegration and universalization (P4) rather than separation (P1), because if the antagonist is a transferable mentality rather than a fixed race, exit from all white persons is no longer the only coherent remedy; correct knowledge and "renewed mind" become sufficient.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Replaces M1's eschatological racial urgency with an urgency of psychological self-correction ("Bring Your Mind In Full Form"), continuous with D3 but now explicitly untethered from D1's racial ontology.

#### PCR Analysis
- **Empirical/Discursive Level.** Carried through a deliberate, extensive retention of the founder's exact vocabulary ("grafted," "Caucasian mentality," "germ," "the Body-Christ") redeployed with a changed referent — evidence of "interpretive succession" rather than declared doctrinal rupture, exactly as W. D. Muhammad describes his own role: a "Mujeddid," "one to watch over the new Islam... to see that it is constantly and continually being renewed." The newly incorporated "Remake the World" and "The Mark of the Beast" articles supply this claim with considerably more direct textual support than the original report's smaller sample.
- **Actual Level.** The frame functions to reposition the movement for external legitimacy without requiring W. D. Muhammad to publicly repudiate his father's founding doctrine — a documented external effect (per contemporaneous press coverage) is the movement's rapid, concrete shift toward normalized relations with civic and financial institutions within months of succession, illustrated inside the corpus itself by the "Remake the World" address's own account of being welcomed to Philadelphia "with a motorcade" and shared platform space with "the Deputy Mayor" and the "chairman of the Commission of Human Relations" — a striking, self-reported contrast with the same minister's account of having previously "met personally when I was here before" only "the police."
- **Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the near-immediate character of this reframing, following directly upon succession, is consistent with a generative mechanism in which succession crisis and institutional-financial pressure (the movement's substantial documented property holdings at the point of transition, detailed in "Accomplishments of the Muslims," 1975) created incentive toward a legitimation strategy compatible with mainstream institutions, while the "germ"/"mentality" vocabulary allowed this shift to be presented as doctrinal deepening rather than doctrinal abandonment. The corpus does not contain internal deliberative documents that would raise this above inference.

**Relative Importance:** **High**, and represents the corpus's second and final instance of full diagnostic transformation by a principal leader (after Malcolm X, 1964) — but, unlike 1964, achieved through authorized succession rather than departure, and therefore without triggering an equivalent to the D4 "hypocrite" delegitimization response.

## COMPARATIVE NOTE: Continuity and Change from Period 1

Frame D3 (mental slavery via religious and educational false consciousness) is the corpus's most direct continuation of a Period 1 frame: Fard's and Elijah Muhammad's 1930s diagnosis of Christianity as "tricknollogy" designed to keep believers "IGNORANT" (*Supreme Wisdom Lessons*; *Final Call to Islam*, 1934) reappears almost unchanged in register across 1957, 1961, 1962, 1964, and 1975 sources, on every side of every later schism, and receives its most elaborated, quasi-clinical restatement yet in W. D. Muhammad's 1975 "subliminal suggestion" discussion. Frame D1 is the direct descendant of the Period 1 Yacub etiology, retained at full doctrinal strength for the entirety of Elijah Muhammad's leadership — and, this expanded edition newly shows, actively and skillfully re-elaborated by Malcolm X himself in at least three distinct public registers between 1960 and 1962 — before being independently abandoned, at different moments and for different reasons, by both Malcolm X and Warith Deen Muhammad. Frames D2, D4, and D5, by contrast, have no clear equivalent in the Period 1 corpus, which shows comparatively little institutional-electoral diagnosis (D2), no documented internal "hypocrite" schism-diagnosis of comparable intensity (D4, though the *Supreme Wisdom Lessons*' "Rebels against the Will of Allah" is a structural precursor), and a lighter, less legally documented state-security presence (D5) than the post-1953, multi-jurisdictional FBI files provide for Period 2. This suggests the 1952–1977 period's diagnostic architecture is best read as the Period 1 cosmology under sustained external pressure (legal, political, generational) that it did not yet face at the movement's founding — and, in its final decade, under sustained internal pressure toward doctrinal modernization that produced D6 as its most consequential single innovation.
