# Prognostic Framing in the Nation of Islam, 1952–1977: A Collective Action Frame Analysis Under Perspectival Critical Realism (Expanded Edition)

## Corpus Note

This is the **prognostic** volume of a three-part frame analysis (Diagnostic / Prognostic / Motivational) of the Nation of Islam corpus, 1952–1977, drawn from the `1952-1977 - Contestation/` directory and selected for centrality to the Elijah Muhammad–Malcolm X contestation and the 1975 Warith Deen Muhammad succession, and for comparability with the Period 1 (1930–1951) prognostic analysis. Sources are cited by short title; the full Works Cited (up to one hundred sources) appears at the end of this document and is shared, with minor emphasis differences, across all three volumes of this analysis.

This expanded edition draws on two bodies of previously uncited material. First, Malcolm X's 1960–1963 public addresses on Elijah Muhammad's behalf (the Harlem Freedom Rally, the Harvard Law School Forum, the Bayard Rustin debate, the Eleanor Fischer interview, the Yale Law School Forum, the UC Berkeley and Columbia University speeches) supply a much fuller, pre-schism record of how P1 (Divine Separation) was actually argued for — its land-fund mechanism, its "some land of our own" formula, and its explicit contrast with both integration and mere token civil-rights gains — establishing that Malcolm X was not merely reciting doctrine he later abandoned but was among the doctrine's most capable public architects for over half a decade. Second, Warith Deen Muhammad's fuller 1975 *Muhammad Speaks* series ("Remake the World," Parts 1–3; "Black America's 'Mission' in the World"; "Weapons"; "Save Your Life"; "The Mark of the Beast"; "A Growth Note: The Church") supplies considerably more textual detail for P4 (Reform and Reintegration) than the original report's smaller sample, including an explicit, dated statement disavowing armed self-defense as an organizational posture — a prognostic data point with no equivalent anywhere else in the corpus. This revision also draws more deeply on the `Elijah Muhammad/Official_Publications` run of *Muhammad Speaks* (1960–1975) for P1's operational land-fund and business-building record.

Prognostic frames answer: *what should be done, and by what mechanism will it work?* Six prognostic frames are load-bearing across the corpus, presented here in more granular and more heavily evidenced form than the earlier edition of this report.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Ranked Prognostic Frames

1. **P1 — Divine Separation** — Elijah Muhammad's stable, unmodified prescription (1957–1974): territorial, economic, and social exit from White America, licensed by Diagnostic Frame D1, and argued for in fullest public form by Malcolm X himself between 1960 and 1963.
2. **P2 — Electoral and Institutional Self-Determination ("The Ballot")** — Malcolm X's brief, sharply articulated prescription of voter registration, bloc voting, and community control of local institutions (March–April 1964).
3. **P3 — Pan-African/Human-Rights Internationalization** — the Organization of Afro-American Unity's prescription, formally organized within eleven weeks of Malcolm X's departure from P2, appealing to international bodies rather than domestic ones.
4. **P4 — Reform and Reintegration** — Warith Deen Muhammad's 1975 prescription of racial de-exclusivity, orthodox Islamic reintegration, and domestic civic legitimation, including an explicit rejection of armed self-organization.
5. **P5 — Armed Self-Defense ("The Bullet")** — the prescription, distinct from but yoked tightly to P2, of community rifle clubs and a reasserted, constitutionally framed right to self-defense, presented as the fallback if P2's mechanism is denied.
6. **P6 — Parallel Black-Nationalist Institution-Building (Muslim Mosque, Inc.)** — Malcolm X's own, short-lived March–June 1964 organizational prescription: a religious-political-economic-cultural base independent of the Nation of Islam but prior to, and distinct from, the internationalist OAAU.

These six prognoses are not presented anywhere in the corpus as compatible variants of one strategy; each is a documented, dated *replacement* of the one before it in the relevant leader's public record. The corpus provides no example of any leader holding two of these prognoses simultaneously as complementary, long-term strategies — though P2, P5, and P6 briefly coexist within the single, compressed eleven-week window between Malcolm X's departure from the Nation of Islam and the founding of the OAAU, and P4 explicitly forecloses P5 by name in 1975.

## DETAILED PROGNOSTIC FRAME ANALYSIS

### P1 — Divine Separation

**Definition.** The prescribed solution to Diagnostic Frame D1 (and, derivatively, D2 and D3) is territorial, economic, and social separation of Black Americans from White America — not reform, not integration, not coexistence.

**Problem Addressed.** D1 directly: if the antagonist is devil-by-nature, no corrective relationship with that antagonist is possible; only exit is coherent. "Separation of the so-called Negroes from their slave-master's children is a MUST. It is the only... Allah, God, is calling for a separation!" (*Message to the Blackman in America*, 1965). *The Fall of America* (1973) restates this as settled prophecy: "The Fall of America Foretold, Separation Is the Answer."

**Mechanism of Change.** The mechanism is theological rather than organizational or political: separation is represented as divinely mandated and prophetically inevitable ("this is the time of our separation and the judgment of this world"), not as a policy outcome contingent on negotiation, legislation, or coalition-building — a critical structural difference from P2, P3, P5, and P6, each of which specifies a concrete organizational or legal lever.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "You must know that this is the time of our separation and the judgment of this world" (MTTBIA, 1965).
- "Watch how anxious the white man is to hold you and call you by his name" (MTTBIA, 1965) — separation is framed as being actively obstructed by the antagonist's self-interested resistance, which reinforces rather than undermines its necessity.
- Continuous with the Period 1 corpus's University of Islam and "get on to your own kind" prognoses (Beynon, 1938; *Final Call to Islam*, 1934), P1 in this period restates rather than innovates on the founding prognosis, extending it into the specific, concrete register of "How to Eat to Live" (1967, 1972) — dietary self-governance as the individual-level installment of the larger collective separation.
- *Muhammad Speaks* itself carries the earliest and most concrete installment of this prognosis as a standing weekly fund appeal, not merely a doctrinal statement: "GIVE THE POOR SO-CALLED NEGROES A CHANCE TO DO FOR SELF! Help Us To Buy Farm Land To Raise Food To Feed The Poor Of Our People... With the purchase of land, wood and open, we can saw our lumber, take clay to make bricks and build our own home" (Vol. 1, No. 2, Dec. 1961; repeated verbatim in Vol. 1, No. 4, Feb. 1962).
- By 1973, Elijah Muhammad restates the same mechanism in his own signed voice, explicitly rejecting dependency as the alternative: "WE, are not going to be a 'lazy Lazarus' lying at the rich man's gate. You will starve to death waiting for crumbs from the white man's table... THE EARTH BELONGS TO US, THE BLACK MAN, but we must prove worthy of it. COME FOLLOW ME. Your Brother, ELIJAH MUHAMMAD, MESSENGER OF ALLAH" (*Muhammad Speaks*, Vol. 12, No. 50, Aug. 1973).
- Malcolm X's own most extended pre-schism public argument for P1's mechanism — delivered years before he would abandon it — comes at the 1960 Harlem Freedom Rally: "We want some land where we can create unity, harmony and brotherhood... we will then set up our own farms, factories, businesses, and schools... and show him how much we appreciate the education he has given us, by using it to become self-sustaining, economically and otherwise" — a fuller, more constructive articulation of the mechanism's intended end-state (a functioning parallel civilization, not merely an exit) than the 1965 and 1973 material alone conveys.
- The same address supplies P1's explicit biblical warrant for the *terms* of separation, distinguishing it from mere abandonment: "Their bible says that when a slave is set free, his slave master should give him something to help him get started on his own... never send him away empty-handed... If the Hebrews in the bible numbered only 600,000... and God was concerned with giving them freedom in a land of their own... then what about 20 million so-called Negroes here in America" — separation is here argued for as restitution, not merely withdrawal, a distinct emphasis from the more purely prophetic register of MTTBIA (1965).
- At the Bayard Rustin debate (Oct. 30, 1961), Malcolm X gives P1's clearest documented statement of *indifference to feasibility* as a rhetorical strategy: pressed repeatedly by Rustin on where the promised territory would actually be and how it would be secured, Malcolm X responds, "The land could be anywhere... Now if the master's intention is good, since we have been faithful workers... then it seems he should give us some of these states" — the mechanism's open-endedness about the specific territory (domestic states versus an African destination) is presented, in this exchange, as a feature rather than a defect, since the theological warrant (restitution owed) is doing the argumentative work, not any specific real-estate proposal.
- At the Yale Law School Forum (Oct. 20, 1962), Malcolm X supplies the corpus's most extended pre-schism justification for *why* Elijah Muhammad, specifically, rather than an established leader, is the proper vehicle for this prognosis: "Will this man of God be someone from Harvard, Yale, Columbia, Howard, or Tuskegee?... God has come to close out the entire Old World... it is now time for God to send us someone with a solution from Himself" — the prognosis's legitimacy is explicitly grounded in the messenger's humble origin, continuous with, and elaborating on, the Moses/Pharaoh's-Magicians analogy used more briefly elsewhere in the corpus.

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Directly derived from D1; largely incompatible with D2 read as a claim to full American citizenship rights, since separation abandons rather than claims the American polity.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Bound tightly to Chosen-Nation/Divine Urgency (M1): separation is not optional strategy but a commanded, time-bound response to prophecy, reinforced by Racial Reversal/Cosmic Status Elevation (M4).

**Empirical Level.** Stated directly and repeatedly, 1957–1974, unmodified over seventeen years across five separate books, and independently and extensively elaborated in at least three separate public addresses delivered by Malcolm X between 1960 and 1962.

**Actual Level.** Functions to justify the Nation of Islam's parallel economic institutions — documented directly in *Muhammad Speaks* and the 1975 "Accomplishments of the Muslims" pamphlet, which itemizes farmland, businesses, and real estate acquired under this prognosis's organizational logic. This is not merely rhetorical: it corresponds to an observable, decades-long pattern of institution-building oriented toward self-sufficient Black economic infrastructure rather than integration into existing white-owned or white-governed institutions.

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the frame's stability alongside genuine, resource-intensive institution-building (documented in the corpus's own economic self-reporting), together with the newly incorporated evidence that its most senior spokesman could argue for it fluently and at length, in front of hostile interlocutors, for at least three years before departing from it, suggests separation functioned as more than rhetoric — consistent with an underlying organizational strategy of building parallel institutional capacity as insurance against both white hostility and internal defection, though the corpus does not contain direct strategic documents confirming this as a stated organizational rationale.

**Relative Importance:** **High** throughout Elijah Muhammad's leadership; **directly reversed**, though not repudiated outright, by his son and successor in 1975 (P4), the single clearest instance of prognostic frame discontinuity in the corpus.

**Organisational Significance.** Separation as prognosis directly organizes movement resources toward land acquisition, farming, and business ownership rather than toward electoral mobilization or coalition politics — a divergence from Malcolm X's post-1964 organizational choices (P2, P3, P6) that is a direct, demonstrable consequence of prognostic disagreement, not merely rhetorical difference.

**Transformational Orientation.**
- *Individual:* required (dietary law, moral discipline) as precondition, not sufficient alone.
- *Collective:* the primary target — the "Black Nation" as a corporate unit to be separated and reconstituted.
- *Institutional:* extensive — parallel schools, farms, businesses.
- *Wider social:* explicitly *not* sought; American society's transformation is framed only as collapse ("the Fall"), never as a reformable object.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MTTBIA (1965); TFOA (1973); HTETLv1/v2 (1967, 1972); "Accomplishments of the Muslims" (1975); MS Vol. 3, No. 4 (1964); MS Vol. 1, No. 2 (Dec. 1961) and Vol. 1, No. 4 (Feb. 1962), "Do For Self" land-fund campaign; MS Vol. 12, No. 50 (Aug. 1973), "Your Brother, Elijah Muhammad" signed appeal; MX_1960_Speech_HarlemFreedomRally; MX_1961_10_30_Debate_BayardRustin; MX_1962_10_20_Speech_YaleLawSchool.

### P2 — Electoral and Institutional Self-Determination ("The Ballot")

**Definition.** Malcolm X's prescribed solution, in the April 1964 "Ballot or Bullet" addresses, is Black voter registration, bloc voting, and community control of local institutions (schools, in particular) as the near-term mechanism of change — explicitly offered as an alternative to, though at that date not yet a full replacement for, separation.

**Problem Addressed.** D2 directly: since the diagnosed problem is institutional exclusion from a functioning democratic mechanism, the prescribed solution is inclusion in and capture of that mechanism.

**Proposed Solution.** Community control of the schools the Board of Education admitted it could not improve; bloc voting to "determine who's going to sit in the White House." "Let them turn those schools over to us. Since they say they can't handle them, nor can they correct them, let us take a whack at it."

**Mechanism of Change.** Explicitly procedural and institutional: expulsion of Dixiecrat committee chairs under existing constitutional machinery ("Any person in Congress right now, who is there from a state or a district where the voting rights of the people are violated, that particular person should be expelled from Congress. And when you expel him... they will be replaced by black representatives from counties and districts where the black man is in the majority"), community takeover of specific underperforming schools, and textbook reform — each identified with a specific institutional lever, not a prophetic timetable.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "It's either a ballot or a bullet... it's time now for you and me to become more politically mature and realize what the ballot is for."
- "A vote for a Democrat is a vote for a Dixiecrat... that's why, in 1964, it's time now for you and me to become more politically mature."
- On the Muslim Mosque, Inc. platform announced weeks earlier: "The political philosophy of black nationalism means: we must control the politics and the politicians of our community. They must no longer take orders from outside forces. We will organize, and sweep out of office all Negro politicians who are puppets for the outside forces" (March 12, 1964).
- The A. B. Spellman interview (March 19, 1964), conducted one week into Malcolm X's independence, shows the electoral-institutional mechanism still developing in real time alongside residual separatist and religious language, evidence that P2's fully secular, procedural register in the April speeches was the product of several additional weeks of refinement rather than an immediate, fully formed replacement for P1.

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Derived from D2 and D3 jointly (institutional exclusion plus miseducation).

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Bound to a *conditional* urgency: "the ballot, or... the bullet" is explicitly framed as contingent on whether the ballot mechanism is allowed to function — a conditional-branching urgency structurally distinct from M1's unconditional prophetic urgency (Motivational Frame M5).

**Empirical Level.** Stated directly, at length, with institutional specificity, in the April 1964 speeches (New York and Cleveland variants).

**Actual Level.** The speech functions to reposition Malcolm X, only weeks after his departure from the Nation of Islam, as a figure addressing a broad, religiously mixed Black audience ("Baptist, or a Methodist, or a Muslim, or a nationalist") on grounds of shared racial interest rather than shared theology — a substantive prognostic and rhetorical departure from anything in the Elijah Muhammad corpus, where audience address is consistently theological.

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the shift from theological to civic-political address, occurring within weeks of the organizational break, is consistent with (not proof of) a generative process in which loss of the Nation of Islam's institutional and doctrinal authority compelled Malcolm X to construct a new basis of address — shared racial-political interest — capable of aggregating a following independent of NOI theology. The corpus does not contain direct first-person testimony from Malcolm X explaining the address shift as strategically necessitated by the break.

**Relative Importance:** **High** for a brief but pivotal period (March–June 1964), superseded within the same year by P3.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1964_03_12_DeclarationOfIndependence; MX_1964_03_19_Interview_ABSpellman; MX_1964_04_03_Speech_BallotOrBullet; MX_1964_04_12_Speech_BallotOrBullet (Cleveland variant).

### P3 — Pan-African/Human-Rights Internationalization

**Definition.** Following the Mecca pilgrimage (April 1964), the prescribed solution shifts from domestic electoral mechanics (P2) toward international human-rights appeal and Pan-African unity, modeled explicitly on the Organization of African Unity (established Addis Ababa, 1963).

**Problem Addressed.** A reformulated version of D2: the problem is redefined from "civil rights" (a domestic legal category) to "human rights" (an international category), explicitly to permit appeal to bodies and audiences outside U.S. domestic jurisdiction.

**Proposed Solution.** "The Organization of Afro-American Unity... patterned after the letter and spirit of the Organization of African Unity"; unification of "all people of African descent in the Western Hemisphere... with our brothers on the African continent." Malcolm X's own account of his travel makes the mechanism explicit: having organized OAAU branches among expatriate Afro-Americans in Ghana and other African countries during his spring and summer 1964 travels, he describes converting "lollygagging" socializing Afro-American expatriates into "pressure groups to get governmental support at the international level against the injustices they were experiencing back home."

**Mechanism of Change.** Coalition-building among African postcolonial states and Afro-diasporic communities to internationalize what had been treated as a purely domestic American question — explicitly analogized to the OAU's success, in Malcolm X's own account, in achieving "independence" and "recognition" for African states in roughly a decade, faster than Black Americans had achieved comparable gains in a much longer span. Malcolm X also documents direct personal diplomacy as part of this mechanism: extended meetings with Presidents Nasser, Nyerere, Kenyatta, Azikiwe, Nkrumah, and Sékou Touré, framed explicitly as building a coalition of heads of state sympathetic to bringing the "Negro problem" before the United Nations. His May 13, 1964 speech at the University of Ghana, delivered mid-tour, extends the mechanism's own account of itself: addressing an audience of African students and expatriates directly, he frames Pan-African unity as flowing in both directions — a diasporic obligation to Africa and an African obligation to the diaspora — rather than as one-directional patronage, distinguishing P3's coalition logic from a simple appeal for African sympathy. His August 25, 1964 letter to the *Egyptian Gazette*, written from Cairo during the second African trip, documents the mechanism's most concrete diplomatic product in the sampled corpus: a direct, publicly circulated appeal to Arab and African opinion asking that the "Afro-American problem" be placed on the agenda of the Cairo summit of the Organization of African Unity, showing P3's international-appeal mechanism operating, in this instance, at the level of an actual diplomatic agenda item rather than only as rhetorical aspiration.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "We determined to try and do the same thing here in America among Afro-Americans... to fight whoever gets in our way, to bring about the complete independence of people of African descent... by any means necessary."
- Mecca letter: "America needs to understand Islam, because this is the one religion that erases from its society the race problem."
- "The only thing power respects is power... The only time he reacts is when he knows you can hurt him" — Malcolm X's account of President Nasser's leverage over Western oil interests, offered as a model for the kind of geopolitical leverage he sought to build for the internationalized movement.

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Explicitly *severed* from D1: the Mecca letter's testimony that "the 'white' Muslims" showed "the same sincerity" as African Muslims is diagnostically incompatible with a devil-by-nature account of whiteness — the corpus's clearest documented instance of diagnostic frame transformation within one individual's public record.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Introduces a new motivational register — universal brotherhood under monotheism (M3) — with no equivalent in Elijah Muhammad's corpus in the sampled period.

**Empirical Level.** Directly stated in the Mecca letter (April 20, 1964) and the OAAU founding charter (June 28, 1964), separated by roughly nine weeks. The University of Ghana speech (May 13), the *Egyptian Gazette* letter (Aug. 25), the Second OAAU Rally (July 5), and the African Summit Conference speeches (Aug. 21) extend and consolidate the same prognosis across the following months, giving the frame a considerably denser documentary record across its ten-month life than the original report's four-source citation conveyed.

**Actual Level.** Functions to reposition the movement's claim-making audience from the U.S. federal government (P2's target) to the community of postcolonial African states and international human-rights bodies — a change in the addressee of the claim, not merely its content. Malcolm X documents the mechanism's early institutional footprint directly: OAAU branches organized in unnamed African countries during his spring travels, then formalized in Paris and London by November 1964, each growing, in his account, "within a very short time... into a well-organized unit."

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the tight nine-week interval between the Mecca letter's diagnostic reversal on whiteness and the OAAU charter's prognostic internationalization suggests the diagnostic change was generative of (causally prior to) the prognostic change, rather than the reverse — the corpus is more consistent with "new direct experience of non-racialized Islam prompted new strategy" than with "new strategic calculation prompted post-hoc theological revision." Malcolm X's own retrospective explanation of the causal order is not present in the sampled documents; this is inference from sequence and content.

**Relative Importance:** **High** for the final ten months of Malcolm X's public life; **absent** from the Elijah Muhammad corpus at any sampled date.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1964_04_20_Letter_FromMecca; MX_1964_05_13_Speech_UniversityOfGhana; MX_1964_06_28_Speech_OAAUFoundingRally; MX_1964_07_05_Speech_SecondOAAURally; MX_1964_08_21_Speech_AfricanSummitConference; MX_1964_08_25_Letter_EgyptianGazette.

### P4 — Reform and Reintegration (Warith Deen Muhammad, 1975)

**Definition.** Upon succession in February 1975, W. D. Muhammad prescribes the dissolution of racial exclusivity in membership and doctrine, reintegration with orthodox international Islam, and a de-emphasis — not full repudiation, in his own careful framing — of confrontational racial rhetoric.

**Problem Addressed.** Reframes D1 as no longer operative: "the past is the past, unless it is dug up. We will not dig it up." The devil-by-nature diagnosis is not argued against point-by-point; it is set aside as belonging to a completed historical phase of the mission.

**Proposed Solution.** "We do not turn to the East for leadership... [but] all Muslims are brothers. Our relationship will be that of one independent community of Muslims with other independent communities of Muslims" — explicit normalization of relations with orthodox Sunni Islam, and, per contemporaneous external press coverage independently corroborating the same shift W. D. Muhammad describes in his own words: "Whites will even be permitted to join the sect" (*Time*, June 1975).

**Mechanism of Change.** Presented as *interpretive succession* rather than doctrinal rupture: W. D. Muhammad frames himself as a "Mujeddid" ("one to watch over the new Islam... to see that it is constantly and continually being renewed"), i.e., the mechanism of change is authorized continuity of interpretive office, not repudiation of the founder. His own doctrinal articles from the same months — "The Mark of the Beast" (March 1975), "Remake the World," Parts 1–3 (April–May 1975), "Artificial Blackness" (June 1975), "The Death of White Supremacy" (July 1975), "Who Is The Original Man?" (Aug. 1975) — perform this mechanism in real time, retaining the founder's full vocabulary ("grafted," "germ," "Caucasian mentality," "the Body-Christ") while systematically redirecting its target from a fixed race to a transferable "colored mind." "Remake the World, Part 1" additionally supplies the mechanism's most explicit statement of *why* institutional reform, rather than continued confrontation, is now the prescribed path: the address opens by publicly thanking a delegation from the Islamic Center in Washington, D.C. and describes being received in Philadelphia by "the Deputy Mayor" and the "chairman of the Commission of Human Relations" — civic contacts the same address contrasts explicitly with his own account of having "met personally when I was here before" only "the police" — presenting reintegration's mechanism as already visibly operative, not merely proposed.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "A Messenger or a Divinely guided man has a responsibility to deliver a message and point to direction and signs. You point out an enemy to focus attention on the enemy. When that attention has been given and the enemy has begun to reform... it would be uncivilized and certainly un-Godly to stay with the finger pointing violently at the enemy."
- "The name Black Muslim... he rejects it as a racist label, but accepts it as an identifying term."
- "Don't waste your time following so-called leaders who guess at solutions or make theories about the problems of our people... Today we are completing the job of growing the baby Nation into manhood and womanhood" ("Artificial Blackness," June 27, 1975).
- "Justice is come and everybody is being put on an equal footing" ("The Death of White Supremacy," July 18, 1975).
- "I am not here to teach hatred, I'm here to kill hatred" ("Remake the World, Part 1," May 16, 1975) — a direct, explicit renunciation of hatred as a program, distinguished in the same breath from any concession that the underlying historical grievance was mistaken: "the slave history and the slave mentality still hangs on in the total makeup of our people. It is a troublemaker in the subconscious. Unless we bring it up so that the conscious can see it and deal with it, we'll still be here a long time in slavery" — reform is thus explicitly presented as compatible with, rather than a denial of, the founder's diagnosis of historical injury.
- "Weapons" (June 13, 1975) supplies P4's single most explicit and operationally significant statement anywhere in the corpus, a direct doctrinal foreclosure of P5 by name: "no follower of Mine is authorized by Me to carry a weapon. We will not allow it because we know what has happened to the Old World... I have not given permission to anybody to arm themselves. That is not My job or My world. I am not here to arm you with physical weapons. I am here to arm you with something superior to physical weapons... Truth and Knowledge." This is the only place in the entire sampled corpus where a principal leader explicitly and by name forecloses the armed-self-defense prognosis (P5) that had circulated, in Malcolm X's public record, since at least late 1963 — direct evidence that P4's "reform" is not merely a softened racial rhetoric but a specific, documented rejection of one of the corpus's other prognostic options.

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Retains D3 (mental/religious captivity) as still operative and requiring correction, while setting aside D1 in its original racial-biological form and replacing it with the psychologized D6 — a selective, not wholesale, diagnostic revision.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Replaces M1's eschatological urgency with an institution-building and legitimacy-seeking motivational register: "Will you seek financial help from the city and state..." "Yes, just as we have already done" — urgency is now directed at the mundane task of institutional survival and civic standing, not apocalyptic timing.

**Empirical Level.** Directly stated across a considerably larger sample of 1975 documents than the original report drew on (First Official Interview, March 21; "I Was Born for This Mission," March 21; "The Mark of the Beast," March 29; "Remake the World," Parts 1–3, April–May; "Weapons" and "Save Your Life," June 13; the June–August doctrinal article series).

**Actual Level.** Functions, observably, to reposition the organization for external legitimacy: the *Time* report (June 1975) independently corroborates concrete effects — invitations to "white civic and church leaders," appeals "for white money" — and the corpus's own "Remake the World" address self-reports the same shift already occurring at the level of local civic reception in Philadelphia within roughly two months of succession.

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the near-immediate (within roughly one hundred days of succession) and externally visible character of this reframing is consistent with a generative mechanism in which succession crisis and institutional-financial pressure (the movement's own reported $14.5 million in Chicago property at the point of transition) created strong incentive toward legitimation strategies compatible with mainstream civic and financial institutions, and the explicit, named foreclosure of P5 ("Weapons") is consistent with the same mechanism operating specifically to remove any organizational liability — legal, reputational, or civic — that an armed posture would have created for a movement actively courting "white civic and church leaders." The corpus does not contain internal deliberative documents (board minutes, private correspondence) that would allow this to rise above inference.

**Relative Importance:** **High**, and represents the corpus's second major instance (after Malcolm X, 1964) of full diagnostic-prognostic frame transformation by a principal leader — but achieved *without* organizational schism, in contrast to 1964.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** IWDM_1975_02_26_Article_SaviorsDayPt2; IWDM_1975_03_21_Interview_FirstInterview; IWDM_1975_03_21_MS_Article_IWasBornForThisMission; IWDM_1975_03_29_MS_Article_TheMarkOfTheBeast; IWDM_1975_05_16_MS_Article_RemakeTheWorld; IWDM_1975_06_13_MS_Article_Weapons; IWDM_1975_06_27_MS_Article_ArtificialBlackness; IWDM_1975_07_18_MS_Article_TheDeathOfWhiteSuprememacy; IWDM_1975_08_22_MS_Article_WhoIsTheOriginalMan; LF_1975_06_25_Article_TimeMagazine; "Accomplishments of the Muslims" (1975).

### P5 — Armed Self-Defense ("The Bullet")

**Definition.** A prognosis distinct from, but tightly yoked to, P2: rather than (or in addition to) capturing existing electoral machinery, Black communities should organize armed self-defense — "rifle clubs" — as an immediate, concrete mechanism for physical protection where the state is "unable or unwilling" to provide it, invoking an existing constitutional right rather than proposing a new one.

**Problem Addressed.** The diagnosed pattern of racial terror and state inaction/complicity (a continuation of the Period 1 corpus's Frame 5, "Racial Terror and Legal Impunity," now updated with named contemporary incidents) combined with D5's government-conspiracy diagnosis: if ordinary legal protection cannot be relied upon, self-organized defense is the only available immediate remedy.

**Proposed Solution.** "In areas where our people are the constant victims of brutality, and the government seems unable or unwilling to protect them, we should form rifle clubs that can be used to defend our lives and our property in times of emergency, such as happened last year in Birmingham; Plaquemine, Louisiana; Cambridge, Maryland; and Danville, Virginia" (Declaration of Independence statement, March 12, 1964).

**Mechanism of Change.** Explicitly framed as legal and constitutional, not extralegal: "it is legal and lawful to own a shotgun or a rifle. We believe in obeying the law... The Constitution of the United States of America clearly affirms the right of every American citizen to bear arms." The mechanism is thus deliberately distinguished from insurrection — it is community-organized, defensive, and triggered specifically by documented failures of state protection, not by an independent political agenda.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "It is criminal to teach a man not to defend himself when he is the constant victim of brutal attacks."
- "When our people are being bitten by dogs, they are within their rights to kill those dogs."
- "We should be peaceful, law-abiding — but the time has come for the American Negro to fight back in self-defense whenever and wherever he is being unjustly and unlawfully attacked."
- "If the government thinks I am wrong for saying this, then let the government start doing its job."
- The religious register of the same prognosis, drawn from Malcolm X's December 1963 "Message to the Grassroots": "There's nothing in our book, the Qur'an... that teaches us to suffer peacefully... an eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth, and a head for a head, and a life for a life: That's a good religion" — showing P5 was articulated in an explicitly religious register even before Malcolm X's formal March 1964 departure from the Nation of Islam.
- Precisely the same fight-back-versus-turn-the-other-cheek register appears already, and even more sharply, in the Eleanor Fischer interview (1961), fully three years before the schism: "I think that any black man who teaches black people to turn the other cheek and suffer peacefully... he is doing those people an injustice, and he's a traitor to his own people... the law of nature gives a man the right to defend himself when he's attacked. And God's law itself gives a man the right to defend himself when he's attacked" — direct, dated evidence, newly incorporated in this expanded edition, that P5's core legal-and-natural-rights argument was fully formed in Malcolm X's public rhetoric years earlier than the 1963–1964 window the original report treated as its point of origin, though not yet organizationally proposed (no "rifle clubs" language appears in the 1961 interview) until the March 1964 Declaration of Independence.
- The same 1961 interview supplies P5's explicit contrast case, aimed specifically at Martin Luther King's program, in terms later echoed but not exceeded by the 1963–1964 material: "peaceful suffering and passive resistance... is all right maybe in India somewhere, where the people in India outnumber the whites... But here in America... you have the mouse now trying to sit down on the elephant."

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Derived jointly from D2 (institutional failure to protect) and D5 (an active, not merely passive, government conspiracy).

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Directly continuous with the sacrificial-witness/courage register (M6) developed fully after the February 1965 firebombing, but present already in nascent form in the 1961 and 1963–1964 speeches' repeated contrast between Black soldiers' willingness to "bleed" abroad and civil-rights nonviolence's demand that they not defend themselves at home.

**Empirical Level.** Stated directly and specifically, with named incidents and named legal citations, in the March 1964 Declaration of Independence statement; in embryonic doctrinal form in the 1961 Eleanor Fischer interview; and in the November 1963 "Message to the Grassroots" speech's extended critique of enforced nonviolence as "novocaine" — a considerably longer documented lineage than the original report's citation window (1963–1965) conveyed.

**Actual Level.** The frame's most direct actual-level referent in the corpus is Malcolm X's own subsequent, lived experience of exactly the kind of undefended vulnerability the prognosis anticipates: the February 1965 firebombing of his own home, an event the corpus documents from his own first-person account delivered the following night. P4's later, explicit foreclosure of this prognosis by name ("Weapons," 1975) is a second, independent actual-level referent: the corpus documents a principal successor leader treating P5 as sufficiently live and organizationally significant, a decade after Malcolm X's departure, to require direct doctrinal prohibition.

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the frame's explicit legal and constitutional framing (rather than a revolutionary or insurrectionary one) is consistent with a generative mechanism in which Malcolm X, newly independent of the Nation of Islam's separatist theology but still committed to a nationalist rather than integrationist politics, sought a prognosis that could be defended within existing American legal categories (the right to bear arms, the right to self-defense) rather than requiring appeal to a wholly external legal or moral order — a middle position between P1's total exit and P2's request for inclusion. The frame's much longer documented rhetorical lineage (from 1961) now visible in this expanded edition suggests the *argument* for self-defense as a natural and religious right predates, and was not generated by, the organizational break; what the break added was only the specific organizational proposal ("rifle clubs") for translating an already-established argument into practice.

**Relative Importance:** **High** as a specific, recurring prognostic thread from at least 1961 through Malcolm X's death, though it is never elaborated into an institution of comparable scale to P1's economic infrastructure or P3's OAAU; it functions throughout as a contingent fallback ("the bullet") rather than a first-choice program, and is the only one of the corpus's six prognoses to be explicitly and by name foreclosed by a later principal leader (P4, 1975).

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1961_Interview_EleanorFischer; MX_1963_11_10_Speech_MessageToTheGrassroots; MX_1964_03_12_DeclarationOfIndependence; MX_1964_04_03_Speech_BallotOrBullet; MX_1965_02_14_Speech_AfterFirebombing_FordAuditorium; IWDM_1975_06_13_MS_Article_Weapons.

### P6 — Parallel Black-Nationalist Institution-Building (Muslim Mosque, Inc.)

**Definition.** Malcolm X's own organizational prescription, announced March 12, 1964, immediately upon leaving the Nation of Islam: a new religious base, the Muslim Mosque, Inc., organized around "black nationalism" as an explicit "political," "economic," "social," and "cultural" philosophy, open to non-Muslim Black participation and to white financial support but not white membership. This prognosis is analytically distinct from both P2 (which addresses existing electoral machinery) and P3 (which is international in scope and organized as the OAAU); P6 is domestic, nationalist, and multi-sector, and it is the immediate, short-lived bridge between Malcolm X's departure and his subsequent, more fully developed prognoses.

**Problem Addressed.** The diagnosed absence, following Malcolm X's departure from the Nation of Islam, of any organizational vehicle at all for his continued political and religious activity — an organizational, not primarily doctrinal, problem.

**Proposed Solution.** "I am going to organize and head a new mosque in New York City, known as the Muslim Mosque Incorporated. This gives us a religious base, and the spiritual force necessary to rid our people of the vices that destroy the moral fiber of our community... Many of our people aren't religiously inclined, so the Muslim Mosque Incorporated will be organized in such manner to provide for the active participation of all Negroes in our political, economic, and social programs, despite their religious or non-religious beliefs."

**Mechanism of Change.** A single institution intended to function simultaneously as a religious congregation, a political vehicle ("we must control the politics and the politicians of our community"), an economic vehicle (open to outside financial support: "The Muslim Mosque Incorporated will remain wide open for ideas and financial aid from all quarters"), and a moral-reform vehicle continuous with the Nation of Islam's own individual-discipline prognosis. Notably, the mechanism explicitly excludes white membership while permitting white financial participation: "Whites can help us, but they can't join us. There can be no black-white unity until there is first some black unity" — a sequencing logic (internal unity as precondition for external coalition) that directly foreshadows, and is later formally superseded by, P3's explicit inclusion of white Muslim solidarity following the Mecca experience only weeks later. The A. B. Spellman interview, conducted one week after the founding announcement, corroborates the mechanism's still-unsettled character at that early date: Malcolm X describes the new mosque's platform in terms almost identical to the March 12 statement, but with noticeably less institutional specificity than the fully elaborated April "Ballot or Bullet" program would soon supply — direct evidence of the frame's rapid development across a period of only a few weeks.

**Representative Quotations.**
- "Our political philosophy will be black nationalism. Our economic and social philosophy will be black nationalism. Our cultural emphasis will be black nationalism."
- "We are completely disenchanted with the old, adult, established politicians. We want to see some new faces — more militant faces."
- "One can't unite bananas with scattered leaves" — the internal-unity-first sequencing logic stated as aphorism.

**Connection to Diagnostic Frames.** Addresses D2 (institutional exclusion) and D4 (the immediate need to establish independent legitimacy following D4-type "hypocrite" attacks anticipated from the Nation of Islam) simultaneously, but does not yet incorporate the D1-reversal that the Mecca letter would introduce five weeks later — P6 retains, at this early date, the separatist premise ("this means that I too believe the best solution is complete separation... to our own African homeland") essentially unmodified from Elijah Muhammad's teaching, even as its organizational form is entirely new.

**Connection to Motivational Frames.** Motivated by an appeal to unity across religious and organizational lines rather than to any single motivational frame: "I'm not out to fight other Negro leaders or organizations. We must find a common approach, a common solution, to a common problem" — a conciliatory register distinct from both M1's cosmic urgency and M2's loyalty/hypocrisy discourse, and one that did not survive the more confrontational tone of the April "Ballot or Bullet" speech only three weeks later.

**Empirical Level.** Directly and fully stated in the March 12, 1964 Declaration of Independence press statement, with a second, corroborating data point supplied a week later by the A. B. Spellman interview — a somewhat fuller empirical base than the original report's single-source citation.

**Actual Level.** The Muslim Mosque, Inc. functioned as Malcolm X's organizational base for barely more than two months before the Mecca pilgrimage (April 1964) generated the diagnostic revision that produced the OAAU (P3) as a second, internationalist organization operating alongside it — the corpus documents P6 being organizationally supplemented, not replaced, since Malcolm X continued to lead the Muslim Mosque, Inc. even after founding the OAAU.

**Real Level (Retroduction).** *Retroductive inference:* the compressed timeline — an organizational announcement (March 12), a still-unsettled restatement of the same platform one week later (March 19), a diagnostic reversal on race (April 20), and a second, internationalist organization within fifteen weeks (June 28) — is consistent with a generative process of rapid, sequential institution-building under conditions of genuine organizational urgency (the need for *some* vehicle immediately upon departure) followed by substantive doctrinal revision as new experience (Mecca) accumulated. The corpus supports this sequence as directly evidenced by dates and content; it does not contain a document in which Malcolm X explicitly narrates the relationship between the two organizations as intentional or planned in advance.

**Relative Importance:** **Medium** — organizationally real and directly evidenced, but analytically overshadowed in the corpus by the more fully elaborated and more doctrinally consequential P3, which supersedes it within the same calendar year without formally dissolving it.

**Evidence Across Corpus:** MX_1964_03_12_DeclarationOfIndependence; MX_1964_03_19_Interview_ABSpellman.

## COMPARATIVE NOTE: Continuity and Change from Period 1

P1 (Divine Separation) is the direct, largely unmodified descendant of the Period 1 corpus's separatist prognosis (Beynon's "get on to your own kind"; the *Final Call*'s symbolic and eventual literal return to Asia/Mecca), now extended into an elaborated economic-institutional program (documented in "Accomplishments of the Muslims") that the smaller, more fragile 1930s movement could not yet sustain, and — this expanded edition now shows in greater depth — actively re-elaborated, at length and with rhetorical sophistication, by Malcolm X himself across at least three separate public forums between 1960 and 1962. P2, P3, P5, and P6 have no direct equivalent in the Period 1 corpus, which shows no comparable engagement with electoral politics, international coalition-building, or a constitutionally framed right to armed self-defense — a reflection of the substantially different political environment (post-1954 civil rights litigation, African decolonization, the existence of the United Nations and the Organization of African Unity as concrete international vehicles) unavailable to the movement's 1930s founders. P4 is the corpus's only genuinely novel prognostic development with no precedent, partial or otherwise, anywhere in the Period 1 material, and marks the point at which the 1952–1977 corpus's prognostic architecture diverges most sharply from its founding decade — a divergence made sharper still by P4's explicit, named foreclosure of P5, an act with no parallel anywhere else in either period's corpus.
